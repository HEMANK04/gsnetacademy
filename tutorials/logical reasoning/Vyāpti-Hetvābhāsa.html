Vyāpti (व्याप्ति) — Invariable Concomitance

Vyāpti = The universal and invariable relationship between the hetu (reason) and sādhya (inferred). It is the foundation of inference (anumāna).

Vyāpti = Universal Relation between Hetu and Sādhya

Key Points:

  • Vyāpti is the logical ground that makes inference possible.
  • It is a necessary relation — wherever hetu is present, sādhya must be present.
  • Without vyāpti, no valid inference can be drawn.
  • It is established through repeated observation (anvaya) and absence (vyatireka).

Example:

  • Vyāpti: Wherever there is smoke, there is fire.
  • This universal relation allows us to infer fire from smoke.
2

Anvaya & Vyatireka — अन्वय एवं व्यतिरेक

  • Anvaya (अन्वय) — Positive Concomitance: The positive relationship — wherever hetu is present, sādhya is also present. (e.g., Where there is smoke, there is fire.)
  • Vyatireka (व्यतिरेक) — Negative Concomitance: The negative relationship — wherever sādhya is absent, hetu is also absent. (e.g., Where there is no fire, there is no smoke.)
Anvaya = Presence of Hetu → Presence of Sādhya
Vyatireka = Absence of Sādhya → Absence of Hetu

Example:

  • Anvaya: In a kitchen, there is smoke and fire (positive instance).
  • Vyatireka: In a lake, there is no fire and no smoke (negative instance).

Together: Both anvaya and vyatireka establish vyāpti.

3

Upādhi (उपाधि) — Condition

Upādhi = A condition that restricts or limits the vyāpti between hetu and sādhya. It is a factor that must be present for the relation to hold.

Upādhi = Limiting Condition of Vyāpti

Key Points:

  • If there is an upādhi, the vyāpti is not universal.
  • A valid vyāpti must be free from upādhi.
  • Upādhi can vitiate (make invalid) the inference.

Example:

  • If we say "Wherever there is smoke, there is fire" — is there any condition? No, it's universal.
  • But if we say "Wherever there is a wet ground, it has rained" — there could be other conditions (sprinkler) → wet ground is not universally concomitant with rain.
4

Hetvābhāsa (हेत्वाभास) — Fallacies of Reason

Hetvābhāsa = A fallacy of the hetu (reason) — when the reason appears to be valid but is actually invalid or defective. It is the Indian logical equivalent of a formal fallacy.

Hetvābhāsa = Defective/False Reason

Five Types of Hetvābhāsa:

  • 1. Savyabhicāra / Anaikāntika — अनियत
  • 2. Viruddha — विरुद्ध
  • 3. Satpratipakṣa — सत्प्रतिपक्ष
  • 4. Asiddha — असिद्ध
  • 5. Bādhita — बाधित
5

Savyabhicāra / Anaikāntika (अनियत) — Irregular/Inconsistent

Savyabhicāra (or Anaikāntika) = The hetu is not uniformly related to the sādhya — it is too wide or inconsistent.

Savyabhicāra = Hetu present where Sādhya is absent

Key Point: The hetu is not invariably concomitant with the sādhya.

Example:

  • Inferring "This is a swan because it is white" — but white things are not always swans (clouds, milk, etc. are white).
  • The hetu "whiteness" is present in many things other than swans → Savyabhicāra.
6

Viruddha (विरुद्ध) — Contradictory

Viruddha = The hetu contradicts the sādhya — it proves the opposite of what is intended.

Viruddha = Hetu proves the Opposite of Sādhya

Key Point: The hetu is inconsistent with the sādhya.

Example:

  • Inferring "This object is permanent because it is produced" — but produced things are impermanent.
  • The hetu "produced" actually proves the opposite (impermanence) → Viruddha.
7

Satpratipakṣa (सत्प्रतिपक्ष) — Counterbalanced

Satpratipakṣa = There is another equally valid reason that proves the opposite conclusion.

Satpratipakṣa = Equal Reason for Opposite Conclusion

Key Point: The hetu is countered by another valid hetu.

Example:

  • Argument 1: Sound is eternal because it is perceptible (like atoms).
  • Counter-argument: Sound is non-eternal because it is produced (like a pot).
  • Both are equally valid → Satpratipakṣa (neither is decisive).
8

Asiddha (असिद्ध) — Unproved

Asiddha = The hetu itself is not established or not accepted — the reason is unproved.

Asiddha = Hetu is Unproved / Not Established

Key Point: The hetu is not known to exist in the pakṣa.

Example:

  • Inferring "This hill has fire because it has smoke" — but if the hill is not known to have smoke, the hetu is asiddha.
  • Inferring "Sound is eternal because it is visible" — sound is not visible, so the hetu is unproved.

Sub-types:

  • Svarūpāsiddha: The hetu itself does not exist in the pakṣa.
  • Viparyayāsiddha: The hetu exists but is not known.
9

Bādhita (बाधित) — Contradicted

Bādhita = The sādhya is contradicted by a stronger valid means of knowledge (pramāṇa).

Bādhita = Sādhya Contradicted by Stronger Pramāṇa

Key Point: The conclusion is already known to be false by a stronger source of knowledge.

Example:

  • Inferring "Fire is cold because it is a substance" — but perception directly tells us fire is hot.
  • The sādhya (fire is cold) is contradicted by direct perception → Bādhita.

Common Exam Traps — सामान्य परीक्षा जाल

  • Trap 1: Vyāpti = anvaya only → ❌ Vyāpti is established by both anvaya (positive) and vyatireka (negative).
  • Trap 2: Upādhi = always a defect → ❌ Upādhi is a condition; if present, it makes vyāpti invalid, but it's not a fallacy itself.
  • Trap 3: Savyabhicāra = contradictory → ❌ It is irregular/inconsistent, not contradictory (that's viruddha).
  • Trap 4: Viruddha = counterbalanced → ❌ It is contradictory (proves opposite). Satpratipakṣa is counterbalanced.
  • Trap 5: Asiddha = already contradicted → ❌ Asiddha = unproved; Bādhita = contradicted.
  • Trap 6: Bādhita = unproved reason → ❌ Bādhita = conclusion contradicted by stronger pramāṇa.
  • Trap 7: Satpratipakṣa = invalid inference → ❌ It means there is an equally valid counter-argument, not that the argument itself is invalid.

Practice Questions — अभ्यास प्रश्न

Question 1 (Vyāpti)

Vyāpti is the relationship between:

  • A. Two pramāṇas
  • B. Hetu and Sādhya
  • C. Two pakṣas
  • D. Two vipakṣas
Answer: B. Hetu and Sādhya

Explanation: Vyāpti is the invariable concomitance between the reason (hetu) and the inferred (sādhya).

Question 2 (Savyabhicāra)

"This is a swan because it is white" — what fallacy does this commit?

  • A. Viruddha
  • B. Savyabhicāra
  • C. Asiddha
  • D. Bādhita
Answer: B. Savyabhicāra

Explanation: Whiteness is present in many things other than swans (clouds, milk) → irregular/inconsistent reason.

Question 3 (Viruddha)

"This object is permanent because it is produced" — what fallacy is this?

  • A. Savyabhicāra
  • B. Viruddha
  • C. Satpratipakṣa
  • D. Asiddha
Answer: B. Viruddha

Explanation: "Produced" actually proves the opposite (impermanence) → contradictory reason.

Question 4 (Satpratipakṣa)

When there is an equally valid reason for the opposite conclusion, which hetvābhāsa occurs?

  • A. Viruddha
  • B. Satpratipakṣa
  • C. Asiddha
  • D. Bādhita
Answer: B. Satpratipakṣa

Explanation: Satpratipakṣa is when an equally strong reason proves the opposite conclusion.

Question 5 (Bādhita)

"Fire is cold because it is a substance" — what fallacy is this?

  • A. Viruddha
  • B. Savyabhicāra
  • C. Bādhita
  • D. Asiddha
Answer: C. Bādhita

Explanation: The conclusion "fire is cold" is contradicted by direct perception (which tells us fire is hot) → Bādhita.

Question 6 (Anvaya & Vyatireka)

The negative concomitance "Where there is no fire, there is no smoke" is called:

  • A. Anvaya
  • B. Vyatireka
  • C. Upādhi
  • D. Vyāpti
Answer: B. Vyatireka

Explanation: Vyatireka is the negative concomitance — absence of sādhya implies absence of hetu.

One-Page Revision — एक पेज में पुनरावृत्ति

Vyāpti = Anvaya (Positive) + Vyatireka (Negative) = Invariable Concomitance

Key Terms:

  • Vyāpti: Universal relation (Hetu ↔ Sādhya)
  • Anvaya: Positive concomitance (Hetu → Sādhya)
  • Vyatireka: Negative concomitance (No Sādhya → No Hetu)
  • Upādhi: Limiting condition of vyāpti

Hetvābhāsa (5 Fallacies of Reason):

  • Savyabhicāra (Anaikāntika): Hetu too wide/inconsistent
  • Viruddha: Hetu proves the opposite
  • Satpratipakṣa: Equal counter-reason
  • Asiddha: Hetu itself is unproved
  • Bādhita: Sādhya contradicted by stronger pramāṇa

Exam Formula:

Vyāpti = Valid Reason | Hetvābhāsa = Defective/False Reason
