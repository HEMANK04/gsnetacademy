Pramā, Apramā, Pramāṇa — प्रमा, अप्रमा, प्रमाण

  • Pramā (प्रमा) — Valid Knowledge: Correct, true knowledge. Knowledge that corresponds to reality.
  • Apramā (अप्रमा) — Invalid Knowledge: Incorrect, false knowledge. Knowledge that does not correspond to reality.
  • Pramāṇa (प्रमाण) — Means of Valid Knowledge: The instrument or source through which valid knowledge (pramā) is obtained.
Pramāṇa → Pramā (Valid Knowledge) | Apramā = Invalid Knowledge

Key Concept: Pramāṇa is the means (source), Pramā is the result (valid knowledge).

Example:

  • Pramāṇa: Seeing the fire (Perception)
  • Pramā: The knowledge that "There is a fire" (Valid knowledge)
  • Apramā: Mistaking a rope for a snake (Illusion — Invalid knowledge)
2

Six Pramāṇas — छह प्रमाण

Indian Philosophy recognizes six main means of valid knowledge (Pramāṇas):

  • 1. Pratyakṣa (प्रत्यक्ष) — Perception
  • 2. Anumāna (अनुमान) — Inference
  • 3. Upamāna (उपमान) — Comparison
  • 4. Śabda (शब्द) — Verbal Testimony
  • 5. Arthāpatti (अर्थापत्ति) — Postulation
  • 6. Anupalabdhi (अनुपलब्धि) — Non-apprehension
Six Pramāṇas = PAUSAA (Pratyakṣa, Anumāna, Upamāna, Śabda, Arthāpatti, Anupalabdhi)
3

Pratyakṣa (प्रत्यक्ष) — Perception

Pratyakṣa = Knowledge obtained through direct sense perception (seeing, hearing, touching, tasting, smelling).

Pratyakṣa = Sense Perception

Key Points:

  • Most fundamental and direct source of knowledge
  • Involves senses (eyes, ears, skin, tongue, nose)
  • Two types:
    • Laukika (Ordinary): Normal sense perception
    • Alaukika (Extraordinary): Yogic/supernatural perception
  • Accepted by all Indian philosophical schools

Example: Seeing the fire directly — "I see the fire burning."

4

Anumāna (अनुमान) — Inference

Anumāna = Knowledge derived through logical inference — from a known sign (hetu) to the unknown (sādhya).

Anumāna = Inference from Sign to Inferred

Key Points:

  • Based on logical reasoning from observed to unobserved
  • Structure: Pakṣa (Subject) → Hetu (Reason) → Sādhya (Inferred)
  • Vyāpti (Invariable Concomitance): The universal relationship between hetu and sādhya
  • Accepted by all Indian philosophical schools

Example:

  • Seeing smoke on a hill → There is fire on the hill.
  • Pakṣa: The hill
  • Hetu: Smoke
  • Sādhya: Fire
  • Vyāpti: Wherever there is smoke, there is fire.
5

Upamāna (उपमान) — Comparison

Upamāna = Knowledge through comparison or similarity between two things.

Upamāna = Knowledge by Comparison/Resemblance

Key Points:

  • Knowledge of an unknown object through its similarity to a known object
  • Involves analogy and comparison
  • Accepted by: Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta
  • Rejected by: Buddhism, Jainism, Sāṃkhya, Yoga

Example:

  • A person who has never seen a gavaya (wild ox) is told it is similar to a cow.
  • When the person later sees a gavaya, they recognize it by comparison to the cow.
6

Śabda (शब्द) — Verbal Testimony

Śabda = Knowledge obtained through words, testimony, or reliable authority (verbal communication).

Śabda = Verbal Testimony / Word

Key Points:

  • Knowledge from trustworthy sources (scriptures, experts, teachers)
  • Two types:
    • Vaidika (Scriptural): Vedic/scriptural testimony
    • Laukika (Worldly): Ordinary trustworthy persons
  • Accepted by: Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta, Sāṃkhya, Yoga
  • Rejected by: Buddhism, Jainism, Vaiśeṣika (as independent pramāṇa)

Example:

  • Learning about historical events from a trustworthy history book.
  • Knowledge of "The Ganges flows from the Himalayas" from a reliable source.
7

Arthāpatti (अर्थापत्ति) — Postulation

Arthāpatti = Knowledge derived by postulation — when a fact cannot be explained without postulating another fact.

Arthāpatti = Postulation / Implication

Key Points:

  • Knowledge of one thing is presupposed to explain another
  • If observed fact cannot be explained without assuming another fact → That fact is known
  • Accepted by: Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta, Vaiśeṣika (some accept)
  • Rejected by: Nyāya, Buddhism, Jainism, Sāṃkhya, Yoga

Example:

  • Observing that a person who is known to be alive is not found in the house → Postulate that the person has gone outside.
  • "Devadatta is fat. He does not eat during the day. Therefore, he must eat at night."
8

Anupalabdhi (अनुपलब्धि) — Non-apprehension

Anupalabdhi = Knowledge through non-perception or absence — knowing that something does not exist.

Anupalabdhi = Knowledge of Absence / Non-perception

Key Points:

  • Knowledge of the absence of something because it is not perceived
  • Direct perception of non-existence
  • Accepted by: Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta (some schools)
  • Rejected by: Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Buddhism, Jainism, Sāṃkhya, Yoga

Example:

  • "There is no pot on the table."
  • Knowing the absence of a pot because one does not see it.

Common Exam Traps — सामान्य परीक्षा जाल

  • Trap 1: Pramāṇa = Pramā → ❌ Pramāṇa is the means; Pramā is the result (valid knowledge).
  • Trap 2: Apramā = invalid means → ❌ Apramā is invalid knowledge, not invalid means.
  • Trap 3: All schools accept all six pramāṇas → ❌ Different schools accept different numbers (2 to 6).
  • Trap 4: Pratyakṣa = only visual perception → ❌ It includes all five senses.
  • Trap 5: Anumāna = only deductive → ❌ It includes both deductive and inductive inference.
  • Trap 6: Upamāna = same as analogy → ❌ It is similarity/comparison specifically.
  • Trap 7: Śabda = only Vedic scripture → ❌ It includes both scriptural and worldly testimony.
  • Trap 8: Anupalabdhi = absence of perception → ❌ It is the knowledge of absence, not just the absence itself.

Practice Questions — अभ्यास प्रश्न

Question 1

Seeing a fire directly is an example of which pramāṇa?

  • A. Anumāna
  • B. Pratyakṣa
  • C. Upamāna
  • D. Śabda
Answer: B. Pratyakṣa

Explanation: Direct sensory perception (seeing) is Pratyakṣa.

Question 2

Inferring fire from smoke is an example of which pramāṇa?

  • A. Pratyakṣa
  • B. Anumāna
  • C. Upamāna
  • D. Arthāpatti
Answer: B. Anumāna

Explanation: Inference from a sign (smoke) to the inferred (fire) is Anumāna.

Question 3

Recognizing a gavaya by comparing it to a cow is an example of which pramāṇa?

  • A. Pratyakṣa
  • B. Anumāna
  • C. Upamāna
  • D. Śabda
Answer: C. Upamāna

Explanation: Knowledge through comparison/similarity is Upamāna.

Question 4

Which school accepts all six pramāṇas?

  • A. Nyāya
  • B. Sāṃkhya
  • C. Mīmāṃsā
  • D. Buddhism
Answer: C. Mīmāṃsā

Explanation: Mīmāṃsā accepts all six pramāṇas: Pratyakṣa, Anumāna, Upamāna, Śabda, Arthāpatti, Anupalabdhi.

Question 5

"Devadatta is fat. He does not eat during the day. Therefore, he eats at night." This is an example of:

  • A. Anumāna
  • B. Arthāpatti
  • C. Upamāna
  • D. Anupalabdhi
Answer: B. Arthāpatti

Explanation: Postulation — the observed fact (fatness) is explained by postulating eating at night.

Question 6

"There is no pot on the table" is an example of which pramāṇa?

  • A. Pratyakṣa
  • B. Anumāna
  • C. Anupalabdhi
  • D. Śabda
Answer: C. Anupalabdhi

Explanation: Knowledge of absence (non-perception) is Anupalabdhi.

Question 7

Which pramāṇa is accepted by all Indian philosophical schools?

  • A. Upamāna
  • B. Śabda
  • C. Pratyakṣa and Anumāna
  • D. Arthāpatti
Answer: C. Pratyakṣa and Anumāna

Explanation: Pratyakṣa (Perception) and Anumāna (Inference) are accepted by all schools.

Six Pramāṇas:

  • Pratyakṣa: Perception (All schools)
  • Anumāna: Inference (All schools)
  • Upamāna: Comparison (Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta)
  • Śabda: Verbal Testimony (Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta, Sāṃkhya, Yoga)

  • Anupalabdhi: Non-apprehension (Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta)

Schools & Count:

  • Nyāya: 4 (Pratyakṣa, Anumāna, Upamāna, Śabda)
  • Vaiśeṣika: 2 (Pratyakṣa, Anumāna)
  • Sāṃkhya/Yoga/Jainism: 3
  • Mīmāṃsā/Vedānta: 6 (All)
  • Buddhism: 2 (Pratyakṣa, Anumāna)

Exam Formula:

Pramāṇa → Pramā (Valid) | Apramā = Invalid | Different schools = Different counts
