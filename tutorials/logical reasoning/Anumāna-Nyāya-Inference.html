Anumāna & Nyāya Inference

Complete tutorial: Anumāna, Pakṣa, Sādhya, Hetu/Liṅga, Vyāpti, Sapakṣa, Vipakṣa, Pakṣadharmatā, Svārthānumāna & Parārthānumāna, Pūrvavat, Śeṣavat, Sāmānyatodṛṣṭa, Kevalānvayi, Kevalavyatireki, Anvayavyatireki, Nyāya Five-Member Syllogism.

Anumāna (अनुमान) — Inference

Anumāna = Knowledge derived through logical inference — from a known sign (hetu/liṅga) to the unknown (sādhya).

Anumāna = Inference from Sign to Inferred

Key Points:

  • Based on logical reasoning from observed to unobserved.
  • Requires Vyāpti (invariable concomitance) — the universal relationship between hetu and sādhya.
  • Accepted by all Indian philosophical schools.
  • Essential for Nyāya logic.

Example:

  • Seeing smoke on a hill → There is fire on the hill.
  • Hetu (sign): Smoke
  • Sādhya (inferred): Fire
  • Vyāpti: Wherever there is smoke, there is fire.
2

Pakṣa, Sādhya, Hetu — पक्ष, साध्य, हेतु

  • Pakṣa (पक्ष) — Subject/Locus: The object or subject about which inference is made. (e.g., The hill)
  • Sādhya (साध्य) — Inferred: The proposition/quality to be proved. (e.g., Fire)
  • Hetu / Liṅga (हेतु/लिङ्ग) — Reason/Sign: The known sign or reason from which inference is drawn. (e.g., Smoke)
Pakṣa (Subject) + Hetu (Sign) → Sādhya (Inferred)

Example:

  • Pakṣa: The hill
  • Sādhya: There is fire
  • Hetu: Because there is smoke
3

Vyāpti (व्याप्ति) — Invariable Concomitance

Vyāpti = The universal and invariable relationship between the hetu (reason) and sādhya (inferred).

Vyāpti = Universal Relation between Hetu and Sādhya

Key Points:

  • Vyāpti is the foundation of inference.
  • It is the invariable concomitance — wherever hetu is present, sādhya is present.
  • Two types:
    • Anvaya-vyāpti: Positive concomitance (Where there is smoke, there is fire)
    • Vyatireka-vyāpti: Negative concomitance (Where there is no fire, there is no smoke)

Example:

  • Anvaya: Wherever there is smoke, there is fire.
  • Vyatireka: Wherever there is no fire, there is no smoke.
4

Sapakṣa & Vipakṣa — सपक्ष एवं विपक्ष

  • Sapakṣa (सपक्ष) — Positive Instance: An example where the sādhya (inferred) is present. It supports the inference.
  • Vipakṣa (विपक्ष) — Negative Instance: An example where the sādhya (inferred) is absent. It helps test the inference.

Example (Smoke → Fire):

  • Sapakṣa (Positive): A kitchen where there is fire and smoke.
  • Vipakṣa (Negative): A lake where there is no fire and no smoke.

Note: For a valid inference, the hetu must be present in the pakṣa, present in all sapakṣas, and absent in all vipakṣas.

5

Pakṣadharmatā (पक्षधर्मता) — Property of the Subject

Pakṣadharmatā = The presence of the hetu (reason) in the pakṣa (subject).

Pakṣadharmatā = Hetu must be present in the Pakṣa

Key Point: This is one of the three conditions for a valid hetu (along with sapakṣa-sattva and vipakṣa-asattva).

Example:

  • Pakṣa: The hill.
  • Hetu: Smoke.
  • Pakṣadharmatā: The hill must have smoke (the hetu must be present on the hill).
  • If the hill does not have smoke, the inference cannot be made.
6

Svārthānumāna & Parārthānumāna — स्वार्थानुमान एवं परार्थानुमान

  • Svārthānumāna (स्वार्थानुमान) — Inference for oneself: The process of reasoning within one's own mind to arrive at a conclusion. It is the internal, personal process of inference.
  • Parārthānumāna (परार्थानुमान) — Inference for others: The formal expression of inference to communicate the reasoning to others. It is the syllogistic, verbal presentation.
Svārthānumāna = Internal Reasoning | Parārthānumāna = Formal Expression

Example:

  • Svārthānumāna: Seeing smoke on a hill and internally concluding "There must be fire."
  • Parārthānumāna: Expressing this as a formal syllogism to convince another person.
7

Pūrvavat, Śeṣavat, Sāmānyatodṛṣṭa — प्रकार

Three types of inference based on the nature of reasoning:

  • Pūrvavat (पूर्ववत्) — Inference from Cause to Effect: Inferring the effect from the cause. (e.g., Seeing dark clouds → It will rain)
  • Śeṣavat (शेषवत्) — Inference from Effect to Cause: Inferring the cause from the effect. (e.g., Seeing a river in flood → It rained upstream)
  • Sāmānyatodṛṣṭa (सामान्यतोदृष्ट) — Inference based on Generalization: Inference based on a general observation or analogy not directly seen. (e.g., Inferring the existence of atoms from the perception of composite objects)
8

Kevalānvayi, Kevalavyatireki, Anvayavyatireki — हेतु के प्रकार

  • Kevalānvayi (केवलान्वयी) — Only Positive: The hetu is present only in positive instances (sapakṣa), and there is no negative instance (vipakṣa) where the hetu is absent. (e.g., "Knowable" is present in all things)
  • Kevalavyatireki (केवलव्यतिरेकी) — Only Negative: The hetu is established only through negative concomitance. (e.g., "Non-different from the self" is known only by absence)
  • Anvayavyatireki (अन्वयव्यतिरेकी) — Both Positive and Negative: The hetu is present in positive instances and absent in negative instances. (e.g., Smoke → Fire; where there is no fire, there is no smoke)
9

Nyāya Five-Member Syllogism — न्याय का पञ्चावयव न्यायवाक्य

The Nyāya school uses a five-member syllogism (Pañcāvayava) for formal inference (Parārthānumāna).

Nyāya Five-Member = Pratijñā + Hetu + Udāharaṇa + Upanaya + Nigamana

Five Members:

  • 1. Pratijñā (प्रतिज्ञा) — Proposition/Statement: The thesis to be proved. "The hill has fire."
  • 2. Hetu (हेतु) — Reason: The reason given for the proposition. "Because it has smoke."
  • 3. Udāharaṇa (उदाहरण) — Example/Instance: A general example supporting the relation. "Wherever there is smoke, there is fire, as in a kitchen."
  • 4. Upanaya (उपनय) — Application: Applying the general rule to the specific case. "The hill has smoke (like a kitchen)."
  • 5. Nigamana (निगमन) — Conclusion: The final conclusion. "Therefore, the hill has fire."
10

Five-Member Example — पञ्चावयव का उदाहरण

MemberSanskritExample
1. Pratijñāप्रतिज्ञाThe hill has fire.
2. HetuहेतुBecause it has smoke.
3. UdāharaṇaउदाहरणWherever there is smoke, there is fire, as in a kitchen.
4. UpanayaउपनयThe hill has smoke (like a kitchen).

11

Comparison & Summary — तुलना एवं सारांश

  • Anumāna: Inference — knowledge from a sign.
  • Pakṣa: The subject/locus of inference.
  • Sādhya: What is to be proved.
  • Hetu/Liṅga: The reason/sign.
  • Vyāpti: Invariable concomitance between hetu and sādhya.
  • Sapakṣa: Positive instance (where sādhya is present).
  • Vipakṣa: Negative instance (where sādhya is absent).
  • Pakṣadharmatā: Hetu must be present in the pakṣa.
  • Svārthānumāna: Inference for oneself (internal).
  • Parārthānumāna: Inference for others (formal syllogism).
  • Pūrvavat: Cause → Effect
  • Śeṣavat: Effect → Cause
  • Sāmānyatodṛṣṭa: Generalization/analogy
  • Kevalānvayi: Only positive hetu
  • Kevalavyatireki: Only negative hetu
  • Anvayavyatireki: Both positive and negative
  • Nyāya Five-Member: Pratijñā → Hetu → Udāharaṇa → Upanaya → Nigamana
12

Common Exam Traps — सामान्य परीक्षा जाल

  • Trap 1: Vyāpti = hetu → ❌ Vyāpti is the universal relation between hetu and sādhya.
  • Trap 2: Pakṣa = the inference itself → ❌ Pakṣa is the subject about which inference is made.
  • Trap 3: Hetu and Liṅga are different → ❌ They are the same (reason/sign).
  • Trap 4: Sapakṣa = where hetu is present → ❌ Sapakṣa is where sādhya is present.
  • Trap 5: Pakṣadharmatā = sādhya in pakṣa → ❌ It is hetu in pakṣa.
  • Trap 6: Svārthānumāna = formal syllogism → ❌ It is internal reasoning; Parārthānumāna is formal.
  • Trap 7: Pūrvavat = effect to cause → ❌ Pūrvavat = cause to effect; Śeṣavat = effect to cause.
  • Trap 8: Nyāya five-member = only three members → ❌ It has five members (Pratijñā, Hetu, Udāharaṇa, Upanaya, Nigamana).
  • Trap 9: Anvayavyatireki = only positive → ❌ It has both positive and negative concomitance.

Practice Questions — अभ्यास प्रश्न

Question 1 (Components)

In the inference "The hill has fire because it has smoke," what is the Hetu?

  • A. The hill
  • B. Fire
  • C. Smoke
  • D. Kitchen
Answer: C. Smoke

Explanation: Hetu is the reason/sign — smoke is the sign from which fire is inferred.

Question 2 (Vyāpti)

"Wherever there is smoke, there is fire" is an example of:

  • A. Pakṣa
  • B. Vyāpti
  • C. Hetu
  • D. Sādhya
Answer: B. Vyāpti

Explanation: Vyāpti is the invariable concomitance between hetu (smoke) and sādhya (fire).

Question 3 (Sapakṣa)

In the inference "Smoke → Fire," a kitchen is an example of:

  • A. Pakṣa
  • B. Sapakṣa
  • C. Vipakṣa
  • D. Hetu
Answer: B. Sapakṣa

Explanation: Sapakṣa is a positive instance where the sādhya (fire) is present.

Question 4 (Types)

Inferring rain from dark clouds is an example of:

  • A. Pūrvavat
  • B. Śeṣavat
  • C. Sāmānyatodṛṣṭa
  • D. Anvayavyatireki
Answer: A. Pūrvavat

Explanation: Pūrvavat is inference from cause (dark clouds) to effect (rain).

Question 5 (Nyāya Syllogism)

What is the third member of the Nyāya five-member syllogism?

  • A. Pratijñā
  • B. Hetu
  • C. Udāharaṇa
  • D. Upanaya
Answer: C. Udāharaṇa

Explanation: The five members are: Pratijñā, Hetu, Udāharaṇa, Upanaya, Nigamana. Udāharaṇa is the third.

Question 6 (Pakṣadharmatā)

Pakṣadharmatā means the hetu must be present in:

  • A. Sapakṣa
  • B. Vipakṣa
  • C. Pakṣa
  • D. Sādhya
Answer: C. Pakṣa

Explanation: Pakṣadharmatā requires that the hetu must be present in the pakṣa (subject).

One-Page Revision — एक पेज में पुनरावृत्ति

Anumāna = Pakṣa + Hetu → Sādhya (based on Vyāpti)

Key Terms:

  • Pakṣa: Subject (e.g., hill)
  • Sādhya: Inferred (e.g., fire)
  • Hetu/Liṅga: Sign (e.g., smoke)
  • Vyāpti: Invariable relation (Smoke ↔ Fire)
  • Sapakṣa: Positive instance (kitchen)
  • Vipakṣa: Negative instance (lake)
  • Pakṣadharmatā: Hetu in pakṣa

Types:

  • Svārthānumāna: For self (internal)
  • Parārthānumāna: For others (formal)
  • Pūrvavat: Cause → Effect
  • Śeṣavat: Effect → Cause
  • Sāmānyatodṛṣṭa: Generalization

Hetu Types:

  • Kevalānvayi (only positive)
  • Kevalavyatireki (only negative)
  • Anvayavyatireki (both)

Nyāya Five-Member:

  • Pratijñā → Hetu → Udāharaṇa → Upanaya → Nigamana

Exam Formula:

Anumāna = Sign (Hetu) + Invariable Relation (Vyāpti) + Subject (Pakṣa) → Inferred (Sādhya)
