Pratyakṣa (प्रत्यक्ष) — Perception

Pratyakṣa = Knowledge obtained through direct sense perception. It is the most fundamental and immediate source of knowledge.

Pratyakṣa = Direct Sense Perception

Key Characteristics:

  • Direct: No intermediary — immediate contact between sense organ and object.
  • Valid: When senses are functioning properly and conditions are normal.
  • Universal: Accepted by all Indian philosophical schools.
  • Foundation: All other pramāṇas ultimately depend on perception.

Example: Seeing a tree, hearing a sound, touching a table.

2

Types of Pratyakṣa — प्रत्यक्ष के प्रकार

  • Laukika (लौकिक) — Ordinary Perception: Normal perception through the five senses under normal conditions. Further divided into:
    • Nirvikalpaka (निर्विकल्पक) — Indeterminate: First moment of perception — object is perceived without any identification or categorization. "Something is there."
    • Savikalpaka (सविकल्पक) — Determinate: Perception with identification — recognizing "This is a tree."
  • Alaukika (अलौकिक) — Extraordinary Perception: Perception beyond ordinary sense limits. Includes:
    • Sāmānyalakṣaṇa: Perception of universal properties
    • Jñānalakṣaṇa: Perception of objects through previous knowledge
    • Yogaja: Yogic/supernatural perception
3

Stages of Pratyakṣa — प्रत्यक्ष के चरण

  • Stage 1: Sense-Object Contact (इन्द्रियार्थ-संयोग): The sense organ comes into contact with the object.
  • Stage 2: Nirvikalpaka (Indeterminate Perception — निर्विकल्पक प्रत्यक्ष): The first, undifferentiated apprehension of the object. No naming or categorization yet. "Something is present."
  • Stage 3: Savikalpaka (Determinate Perception — सविकल्पक प्रत्यक्ष): The object is identified, named, and categorized. "This is a tree."
  • Stage 4: Judgment/Recognition: The perceived object is related to previous knowledge and experience.

Example:

  • Nirvikalpaka: Seeing something brown and tall (just sensation).
  • Savikalpaka: Recognizing "This is a mango tree."

Distinction:

  • Nirvikalpaka = Preconceptual — बिना विकल्प के
  • Savikalpaka = Conceptual — विकल्प सहित
4

Upamāna (उपमान) — Comparison

Upamāna = Knowledge obtained through comparison or resemblance between two objects.

Upamāna = Knowledge by Similarity/Comparison

Key Characteristics:

  • Knowledge of an unknown object through its similarity to a known object.
  • Involves analogy and comparison.
  • Accepted by: Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta.
  • Rejected by: Buddhism, Jainism, Sāṃkhya, Yoga.

Example:

  • A person who has never seen a gavaya (wild ox) is told it is similar to a cow.
  • When the person later sees a gavaya, they recognize it by comparing it to the cow.
  • Knowledge: "This is a gavaya, similar to a cow."
5

Śabda (शब्द) — Verbal Testimony

Śabda = Knowledge obtained through words, testimony, or reliable authority.

Śabda = Verbal Testimony / Word

Key Characteristics:

  • Knowledge from trustworthy sources (scriptures, experts, teachers).
  • Two types:
    • Vaidika (वैदिक) — Scriptural: Vedic/scriptural testimony.
    • Laukika (लौकिक) — Worldly: Ordinary trustworthy persons.
  • Accepted by: Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta, Sāṃkhya, Yoga.
  • Rejected by: Buddhism, Jainism, Vaiśeṣika (as independent pramāṇa).

Example:

  • Learning about historical events from a trustworthy history book.
  • "The Ganges flows from the Himalayas" — knowledge from a reliable source.
6

Āpta (आप्त) — Reliable Authority

Āpta = A trustworthy, reliable person whose testimony can be accepted as valid knowledge.

Āpta = Reliable / Trustworthy Authority

Characteristics of Āpta:

  • Direct knowledge (Sākṣāt-kṛta-dharma): Has direct knowledge or access to truth.
  • Free from bias (Anāśrita): Not influenced by personal desires or prejudices.
  • Truthful (Satya-vādin): Always speaks the truth.
  • Free from defects (Doṣa-rahita): Free from attachment, aversion, and ignorance.

Types of Āpta:

  • Āpta in Vedic context: Rishis (sages) who directly perceived the Vedic truths.
  • Āpta in worldly context: Trustworthy experts, teachers, or reliable individuals.

Example:

  • A trusted medical doctor's diagnosis is accepted as Śabda (verbal testimony).
  • A teacher's statement about a historical event.
7

Arthāpatti (अर्थापत्ति) — Postulation

Arthāpatti = Knowledge derived by postulation — when a fact cannot be explained without assuming another fact.

Arthāpatti = Postulation / Implication

Key Characteristics:

  • If observed fact cannot be explained without assuming another fact → That fact is known.
  • It is presupposition — something must be assumed to make sense of what is observed.
  • Accepted by: Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta (some accept).
  • Rejected by: Nyāya, Buddhism, Jainism, Sāṃkhya, Yoga.

Example 1:

  • Observing that a person who is known to be alive is not found in the house → Postulate that the person has gone outside.
  • Known: Devadatta is alive and not in the house.
  • Postulation: Devadatta must be outside.

Example 2:

  • "Devadatta is fat. He does not eat during the day. Therefore, he must eat at night."
  • The fatness cannot be explained without postulating night eating.
8

Anupalabdhi (अनुपलब्धि) — Non-apprehension

Anupalabdhi = Knowledge through non-perception or absence — knowing that something does not exist.

Anupalabdhi = Knowledge of Absence / Non-perception

Key Characteristics:

  • Knowledge of the absence of something because it is not perceived.
  • Direct perception of non-existence.
  • Also known as Abhāva (अभाव) — non-existence.
  • Accepted by: Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta (some schools).
  • Rejected by: Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Buddhism, Jainism, Sāṃkhya, Yoga.

Example:

  • "There is no pot on the table."
  • Knowing the absence of a pot because one does not see it.
  • "There is no sound in this room." (Knowing silence — absence of sound)

Common Exam Traps — सामान्य परीक्षा जाल

  • Trap 1: Pratyakṣa = only visual perception → ❌ It includes all five senses.
  • Trap 2: Nirvikalpaka = perception of the object's name → ❌ It is perception without any identification/name.
  • Trap 3: Savikalpaka = the first moment of perception → ❌ Savikalpaka comes after Nirvikalpaka.
  • Trap 4: Upamāna = any comparison → ❌ It is specifically knowledge of an unknown object through similarity to a known object.
  • Trap 5: Śabda = only Vedic testimony → ❌ It includes both scriptural and worldly testimony.
  • Trap 6: Āpta = any person → ❌ Āpta must be reliable, truthful, and free from defects.
  • Trap 7: Arthāpatti = inference (anumāna) → ❌ Arthāpatti is postulation/presupposition, different from inference.
  • Trap 8: Anupalabdhi = just absence → ❌ It is the knowledge of absence, not just the absence itself.
  • Trap 9: All schools accept all five pramāṇas → ❌ Different schools accept different pramāṇas.

Practice Questions — अभ्यास प्रश्न

Question 1 (Pratyakṣa Types)

Recognizing "This is a rose" is an example of which type of perception?

  • A. Nirvikalpaka
  • B. Savikalpaka
  • C. Yogaja
  • D. Sāmānyalakṣaṇa
Answer: B. Savikalpaka

Explanation: Savikalpaka is determinate perception with identification and categorization.

Question 2 (Upamāna)

Recognizing a gavaya by comparing it to a cow is an example of:

  • A. Pratyakṣa
  • B. Anumāna
  • C. Upamāna
  • D. Śabda
Answer: C. Upamāna

Explanation: Knowledge through comparison/similarity is Upamāna.

Question 3 (Śabda & Āpta)

Knowledge from a trustworthy teacher is an example of which pramāṇa?

  • A. Pratyakṣa
  • B. Anumāna
  • C. Upamāna
  • D. Śabda
Answer: D. Śabda

Explanation: Verbal testimony from a reliable source is Śabda.

Question 4 (Arthāpatti)

"Devadatta is fat. He does not eat during the day. Therefore, he eats at night." This is:

  • A. Anumāna
  • B. Arthāpatti
  • C. Upamāna
  • D. Anupalabdhi
Answer: B. Arthāpatti

Explanation: Postulation — explaining the observed fact (fatness) by postulating night eating.

Question 5 (Anupalabdhi)

Knowing "There is no water in this glass" is an example of:

  • A. Pratyakṣa
  • B. Anumāna
  • C. Anupalabdhi
  • D. Śabda
Answer: C. Anupalabdhi

Explanation: Knowledge of absence through non-perception is Anupalabdhi.

Question 6 (Stages)

The first moment of perception without any identification is called:

  • A. Savikalpaka
  • B. Nirvikalpaka
  • C. Laukika
  • D. Alaukika
Answer: B. Nirvikalpaka

Explanation: Nirvikalpaka is indeterminate perception before identification.

One-Page Revision — एक पेज में पुनरावृत्ति

Pratyakṣa = Direct Perception | Upamāna = Comparison | Śabda = Testimony | Arthāpatti = Postulation | Anupalabdhi = Non-apprehension

Pratyakṣa Types:

  • Laukika (Ordinary): Nirvikalpaka (Indeterminate) → Savikalpaka (Determinate)
  • Alaukika (Extraordinary): Sāmānyalakṣaṇa, Jñānalakṣaṇa, Yogaja

Śabda & Āpta:

  • Śabda = Verbal testimony (Vaidika + Laukika)
  • Āpta = Reliable authority — truthful, unbiased, has direct knowledge

Arthāpatti:

  • Postulation — explaining an observed fact by assuming another fact

Anupalabdhi:

  • Knowledge of absence/non-existence

Exam Formula:

Pratyakṣa = Direct | Upamāna = Similarity | Śabda = Words | Arthāpatti = Postulation | Anupalabdhi = Absence
