Greenhouse Effect — ग्रीनहाउस प्रभाव

Greenhouse Effect वह natural process है जिसमें greenhouse gases Earth's atmosphere में heat को trap करते हैं — Earth को warm and habitable बनाते हैं।

Greenhouse Effect = Sun's Energy + Earth's Radiation + GHG Trap Heat

How it Works:

  • Incoming solar radiation: Sun's energy reaches Earth
  • Earth absorbs and re-emits: Earth's surface warms and emits infrared radiation (heat)
  • GHG trap heat: Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, trapping heat in the atmosphere
  • Natural vs Enhanced: Natural greenhouse effect is essential for life; enhanced effect (due to human activities) causes global warming
2

Greenhouse Gases (GHGs) — ग्रीनहाउस गैसें

Greenhouse Gases वे gases हैं जो infrared radiation को absorb और re-emit करती हैं — causing the greenhouse effect.

Major GHGs:

  • CO₂ (Carbon Dioxide) — कार्बन डाइऑक्साइड: Most abundant GHG; from fossil fuel burning, deforestation
  • CH₄ (Methane) — मीथेन: 25x more potent than CO₂; from agriculture, livestock, landfills
  • N₂O (Nitrous Oxide) — नाइट्रस ऑक्साइड: 298x more potent than CO₂; from agriculture (fertilisers), industrial processes
  • CFCs (Chlorofluorocarbons) — CFC: Ozone-depleting and potent GHGs; from refrigerants, aerosols
  • Water Vapour (H₂O) — जल वाष्प: Most abundant GHG; natural
  • Ozone (O₃) — ओजोन: Tropospheric ozone is a GHG

GWP (Global Warming Potential):

  • CO₂ = 1 (baseline)
  • CH₄ = 25
  • N₂O = 298
  • CFCs = Thousands

Global Warming — वैश्विक तापन

Global Warming Earth's average temperature में long-term increase है — primarily due to enhanced greenhouse effect caused by human activities.

Global Warming = Enhanced GHG + Trapped Heat + Rising Temperature

Causes:

  • Burning of fossil fuels (CO₂ emissions)
  • Deforestation (reduces CO₂ absorption)
  • Agriculture and livestock (CH₄, N₂O)
  • Industrial processes (GHG emissions)
  • Waste decomposition (CH₄)

Effects:

  • Rising global temperatures
  • Melting ice caps and glaciers
  • Sea level rise
  • Extreme weather events
  • Ocean acidification
4

Climate Change — जलवायु परिवर्तन

Climate Change weather patterns में long-term shift है — temperature, precipitation, wind patterns में changes — primarily due to global warming.

Climate Change = Global Warming + Weather Pattern Shifts + Long-term Changes

Difference: Global Warming vs Climate Change

  • Global Warming: Increase in average global temperature
  • Climate Change: Broader concept — includes temperature changes + precipitation, wind, storms, etc.

Key Observations:

  • Rising global average temperature
  • Changing precipitation patterns
  • Increasing frequency of extreme weather
  • Melting ice and rising sea levels
5

Climate Change Impacts — जलवायु परिवर्तन के प्रभाव

  • Environmental Impacts:
    • Melting glaciers and ice sheets
    • Rising sea levels
    • Ocean acidification
    • Loss of biodiversity
    • Changes in ecosystems and habitats
  • Social/Economic Impacts:
    • Agriculture and food security
    • Water scarcity
    • Health impacts (heat stress, disease spread)
    • Climate refugees
    • Economic losses (damage to infrastructure, reduced productivity)
  • Extreme Weather Events:
    • More frequent and intense heatwaves
    • Heavy rainfall and floods
    • Droughts and wildfires
    • Stronger cyclones and storms

Mitigation & Adaptation — शमन एवं अनुकूलन

  • Mitigation — शमन: Greenhouse gas emissions को reduce या prevent करना — addressing the cause of climate change.
    • Renewable energy (solar, wind)
    • Energy efficiency
    • Reforestation and afforestation
    • Carbon capture and storage
    • Reducing deforestation
    • Transition to low-carbon economy
  • Adaptation — अनुकूलन: Climate change के effects को manage करना — adjusting to the consequences.
    • Building flood defences
    • Drought-resistant crops
    • Early warning systems
    • Climate-resilient infrastructure
    • Water conservation
7

Climate Justice — जलवायु न्याय

Climate Justice का अर्थ है कि climate change के impacts और responsibilities को fairly distributed किया जाए — especially for vulnerable and marginalised communities.

Climate Justice = Fair Distribution + Vulnerable Protection + Equitable Responsibility

Key Points:

  • Climate change affects poor and vulnerable communities the most
  • Developed countries have contributed most emissions
  • Developing countries have limited resources to adapt
  • Climate justice demands equity in mitigation and adaptation
  • Includes intergenerational and intragenerational equity
8

CBDR-RC — समान परंतु विभेदित उत्तरदायित्व

Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC) — climate action में all countries have common responsibility, but differentiated based on their historical emissions and capabilities.

CBDR-RC = Common Responsibility + Differentiated Action + Respective Capabilities

Key Points:

  • All countries must act on climate change
  • Developed countries have greater responsibility (historically emitted more)
  • Developing countries have less capacity and need support
  • Developed countries should provide climate finance and technology transfer
  • Included in UNFCCC and Paris Agreement

Ozone Depletion — ओजोन क्षरण

Ozone Depletion stratospheric ozone layer का thinning है — primarily caused by CFCs (Chlorofluorocarbons) and other ozone-depleting substances (ODS).

Ozone Depletion = CFCs + UV Radiation + Ozone Layer Thinning

Key Points:

  • Ozone layer: Protects Earth from harmful UV-B radiation
  • CFCs: Used in refrigerants, aerosols, solvents
  • Other ODS: Halons, methyl bromide, carbon tetrachloride
  • Ozone hole: Thinning over Antarctica (discovered 1985)

Effects:

  • Increased UV radiation reaches Earth
  • Skin cancer and cataracts in humans
  • Damage to plants and marine ecosystems

Montreal Protocol (1987):

  • International treaty to phase out ODS
  • Most successful environmental treaty
  • Ozone layer is recovering

Ocean Acidification — महासागरीय अम्लीकरण

Ocean Acidification ocean water का pH decrease है — due to absorption of atmospheric CO₂.

Ocean Acidification = CO₂ + Ocean + pH Decrease

How it Works:

  • Atmospheric CO₂ dissolves in ocean water
  • Forms carbonic acid (H₂CO₃)
  • Releases H⁺ ions → pH decreases (more acidic)
  • Impacts marine organisms

Effects:

  • Shell-forming organisms (corals, shellfish, plankton) — their calcium carbonate shells dissolve
  • Coral bleaching and reef degradation
  • Disruption of marine food webs
  • Reduced fish populations
11

Biodiversity Loss — जैव विविधता हानि

Biodiversity Earth पर life की variety है — genes, species, ecosystems. Biodiversity Loss इस variety का decline है.

Biodiversity = Species + Genes + Ecosystems | Loss = Decline in Variety

Causes:

  • Habitat destruction — आवास विनाश: Deforestation, urbanisation, agriculture
  • Climate Change — जलवायु परिवर्तन: Alters habitats and ranges
  • Pollution — प्रदूषण: Air, water, soil pollution
  • Overexploitation — अत्यधिक दोहन: Overfishing, hunting, poaching
  • Invasive Species — आक्रामक प्रजातियाँ: Non-native species disrupting ecosystems

Impacts:

  • Loss of ecosystem services
  • Food and water insecurity
  • Medicinal resource loss
  • Ecosystem instability

International Agreements — अंतर्राष्ट्रीय समझौते

  • UNFCCC (1992) — संयुक्त राष्ट्र जलवायु परिवर्तन फ्रेमवर्क कन्वेंशन: Framework for climate change action; Rio Summit 1992; CBDR principle
  • Kyoto Protocol (1997) — क्योटो प्रोटोकॉल: Legally binding emission reduction targets for developed countries (Annex I countries)
  • Paris Agreement (2015) — पेरिस समझौता: Global climate agreement — all countries; goal to limit warming to 1.5°C-2°C; NDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions)
  • Montreal Protocol (1987) — मॉन्ट्रियल प्रोटोकॉल: Phase out ozone-depleting substances (CFCs)
  • CBD (1992) — Convention on Biological Diversity: Conservation of biodiversity, sustainable use, equitable sharing of benefits

Common Exam Traps — सामान्य परीक्षा जाल

  • Trap 1: Global warming = climate change → ❌ Global warming is one aspect of climate change.
  • Trap 2: Ozone depletion = global warming → ❌ Ozone depletion is caused by CFCs; global warming is caused by GHGs.
  • Trap 3: CO₂ is the most potent GHG → ❌ CO₂ is most abundant but less potent than CH₄, N₂O, CFCs.
  • Trap 4: Ocean acidification = ocean pollution → ❌ Ocean acidification is caused by CO₂ absorption, not pollution.
  • Trap 5: CBDR = all countries equal responsibility → ❌ CBDR means differentiated responsibility based on historical emissions.
  • Trap 6: Montreal Protocol = climate change → ❌ Montreal Protocol is for ozone depletion (CFCs).
  • Trap 7: Paris Agreement = legally binding targets → ❌ Paris Agreement uses NDCs (voluntary, not legally binding targets).

Practice Questions — अभ्यास प्रश्न

Question 1

Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?

  • A. CO₂
  • B. CH₄
  • C. O₂
  • D. N₂O
Answer: C. O₂

Explanation: O₂ (oxygen) is not a greenhouse gas. CO₂, CH₄, and N₂O are GHGs.

Question 2

The Montreal Protocol is associated with:

  • A. Climate change
  • B. Ozone depletion
  • C. Biodiversity
  • D. Desertification
Answer: B. Ozone depletion

Explanation: The Montreal Protocol (1987) was designed to phase out ozone-depleting substances (CFCs).

Question 3

CBDR-RC is a principle associated with:

  • A. Ozone depletion
  • B. Climate change
  • C. Biodiversity
  • D. Desertification
Answer: B. Climate change

Explanation: CBDR-RC (Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities) is a key principle of climate change governance (UNFCCC, Paris Agreement).

Question 4

Which gas has the highest Global Warming Potential (GWP)?

  • A. CO₂
  • B. CH₄
  • C. N₂O
  • D. CFCs
Answer: D. CFCs

Explanation: CFCs have the highest GWP (thousands of times more potent than CO₂). N₂O is 298x, CH₄ is 25x, CO₂ is 1x.

Question 5

Ocean acidification is caused by:

  • A. Excess nutrients
  • B. CO₂ absorption
  • C. Oil spills
  • D. Plastic waste
Answer: B. CO₂ absorption

Explanation: Ocean acidification is caused by the absorption of atmospheric CO₂, which forms carbonic acid and lowers pH.

Question 6

The Paris Agreement was adopted in:

  • A. 1992
  • B. 1997
  • C. 2005
  • D. 2015
Answer: D. 2015

Explanation: The Paris Agreement was adopted in 2015 (entered into force in 2016).
