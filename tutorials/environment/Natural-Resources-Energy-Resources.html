Natural Resources — प्राकृतिक संसाधन

Natural Resources वे materials और substances हैं जो nature में naturally पाए जाते हैं और humans द्वारा use किए जाते हैं।

Natural Resources = Materials from Nature + Human Use

Classification:

  • Renewable — नवीकरणीय: Can be replenished naturally (solar, wind, water, biomass)
  • Non-renewable — अनवीकरणीय: Finite, cannot be replenished (fossil fuels, minerals, nuclear)
  • Biotic — जैविक: Living resources (forests, animals)
  • Abiotic — अजैविक: Non-living resources (water, minerals, air)

Solar Energy — सौर ऊर्जा

Solar Energy sun से प्राप्त energy है — captured through solar panels (PV cells) या solar thermal systems.

  • Source: Sun
  • Technology: Photovoltaic (PV) cells, Solar thermal collectors
  • Advantages: Clean, abundant, renewable, low operating cost
  • Limitations: Intermittent (depends on sunlight), requires storage, high initial cost
  • Applications: Electricity generation, water heating, cooking, lighting
4

Wind Energy — पवन ऊर्जा

Wind Energy wind की kinetic energy को wind turbines के माध्यम से electricity में convert करती है।

  • Source: Wind
  • Technology: Wind turbines (onshore, offshore)
  • Advantages: Clean, renewable, low operating cost, no emissions
  • Limitations: Intermittent (wind dependent), visual and noise impact, bird mortality
  • Applications: Electricity generation, water pumping
5

Hydropower — जल विद्युत

Hydropower flowing or falling water की energy को electricity में convert करती है — using dams or run-of-river systems.

  • Source: Flowing/falling water
  • Technology: Hydroelectric dams, pumped storage
  • Advantages: Reliable, renewable, low operating cost, flood control (dams)
  • Limitations: Environmental impact (displacement, ecosystem disruption), high initial cost, sedimentation
  • Applications: Electricity generation, irrigation
6

Geothermal Energy — भूतापीय ऊर्जा

Geothermal Energy Earth's interior से heat का उपयोग करती है — from geothermal reservoirs.

  • Source: Earth's internal heat
  • Technology: Geothermal power plants, geothermal heat pumps
  • Advantages: Reliable, constant (baseload), low emissions, small footprint
  • Limitations: Location-specific (volcanic/geothermal areas), high initial cost, potential for induced seismicity
  • Applications: Electricity generation, heating, cooling
7

Biomass Energy — जैव-ईंधन/बायोमास

Biomass Energy organic matter (plants, agricultural waste, wood) से प्राप्त energy है — through combustion, gasification, or biogas.

  • Source: Plant material, agricultural residue, animal waste
  • Technology: Combustion, anaerobic digestion (biogas), pyrolysis
  • Advantages: Renewable, uses waste, carbon-neutral (if sustainable)
  • Limitations: Can cause deforestation, air pollution, land use conflict
  • Applications: Electricity generation, heating, cooking, biogas (methane)
8

Nuclear Energy — नाभिकीय ऊर्जा

Nuclear Energy atomic nuclei के fission (splitting) या fusion (combining) से release होने वाली energy है — used in nuclear power plants.

  • Source: Uranium, Plutonium (fission); Hydrogen (fusion)
  • Technology: Nuclear fission reactors
  • Advantages: High energy density, low GHG emissions, reliable (baseload)
  • Limitations: Radioactive waste disposal, nuclear accidents (Chernobyl, Fukushima), high cost, non-renewable
  • Applications: Electricity generation, medical isotopes, research
9

Forest Resources — वन संसाधन

Forest Resources forests से प्राप्त resources हैं — wood, timber, medicinal plants, wildlife, ecosystem services.

  • Types: Tropical, Temperate, Boreal (Taiga)
  • Uses: Timber, fuelwood, paper, medicines, fodder, habitat
  • Ecosystem Services: Carbon sequestration, oxygen production, water cycle regulation, soil conservation, biodiversity
  • Threats: Deforestation, degradation, forest fires, climate change
  • Conservation: Afforestation, reforestation, sustainable forestry, protected areas
10

Soil Resources — मृदा संसाधन

Soil Earth's surface पर एक natural resource है — supports plant growth, filters water, and provides habitat.

  • Components: Minerals, organic matter, water, air, living organisms
  • Types: Alluvial, Black, Red, Laterite, Desert, Mountain
  • Importance: Food production, water filtration, nutrient cycling, biodiversity
  • Threats: Soil erosion, degradation, desertification, pollution, salinisation
  • Conservation: Contour ploughing, terracing, afforestation, organic farming, crop rotation
11

Water Resources — जल संसाधन

Water Resources Earth's water is available in various forms — freshwater (rivers, lakes, groundwater) and saltwater (oceans).

  • Sources: Surface water (rivers, lakes), Groundwater (aquifers), Rainwater, Oceans (desalination)
  • Importance: Drinking, irrigation, industry, sanitation, hydropower, ecosystem
  • Threats: Overexploitation (groundwater depletion), pollution, climate change, water scarcity
  • Conservation: Rainwater harvesting, drip irrigation, water recycling, watershed management
12

Resource Conservation — संसाधन संरक्षण

Resource Conservation resources का wise and sustainable use है — to ensure their availability for future generations.

Resource Conservation = Sustainable Use + Reduce + Reuse + Recycle

Principles:

  • Reduce — कम करें: Minimise consumption
  • Reuse — पुन:उपयोग करें: Use items multiple times
  • Recycle — पुनर्चक्रण करें: Process waste into new products
  • Renewable — नवीकरणीय: Use renewable resources where possible
  • Protection — संरक्षण: Protect resources from degradation

Methods:

  • Energy conservation (efficiency, renewables)
  • Water conservation (rainwater harvesting, drip irrigation)
  • Soil conservation (terracing, afforestation)
  • Forest conservation (afforestation, protected areas)
  • Waste management (reduce, reuse, recycle)

Common Exam Traps — सामान्य परीक्षा जाल

  • Trap 1: Nuclear energy is renewable → ❌ Nuclear energy is non-renewable (uranium is finite).
  • Trap 2: Biomass is carbon-neutral → ✅ Only if sustainably managed; otherwise, it can release CO₂.
  • Trap 3: Hydropower has no environmental impact → ❌ Dams cause displacement, ecosystem disruption.
  • Trap 4: Geothermal energy is available everywhere → ❌ It is location-specific (volcanic/geothermal areas).
  • Trap 5: Solar energy is available 24/7 → ❌ Solar is intermittent (daytime only).
  • Trap 6: Wind energy produces no waste → ❌ Turbines have waste (blades, etc.), and visual/noise impact.
  • Trap 7: All forests are renewable → ❌ Forests can be non-renewable if overexploited faster than regeneration.

Practice Questions — अभ्यास प्रश्न

Question 1

Which of the following is a non-renewable energy source?

  • A. Solar
  • B. Wind
  • C. Nuclear
  • D. Geothermal
Answer: C. Nuclear

Explanation: Nuclear energy (uranium) is non-renewable. Solar, wind, and geothermal are renewable.

Question 2

Which renewable energy source is considered location-specific?

  • A. Solar
  • B. Wind
  • C. Geothermal
  • D. Biomass
Answer: C. Geothermal

Explanation: Geothermal energy is location-specific (requires volcanic/geothermal areas).

Question 3

Which of the following is a method of soil conservation?

  • A. Deforestation
  • B. Overgrazing
  • C. Contour ploughing
  • D. Monoculture
Answer: C. Contour ploughing

Explanation: Contour ploughing (ploughing along contour lines) helps prevent soil erosion. Deforestation, overgrazing, and monoculture can cause soil degradation.

Question 4

Biomass energy is derived from:

  • A. Sunlight
  • B. Organic matter
  • C. Wind
  • D. Water
Answer: B. Organic matter

Explanation: Biomass energy comes from organic matter (plants, agricultural waste, animal waste).

Question 5

Which of the following is a limitation of hydropower?

  • A. Low energy density
  • B. Intermittent supply
  • C. Environmental impact (dams)
  • D. High GHG emissions
Answer: C. Environmental impact (dams)

Explanation: Dams cause environmental impacts (displacement, ecosystem disruption). Hydropower is reliable and has low GHG emissions.

Question 6

Which principle is NOT part of resource conservation?

  • A. Reduce
  • B. Reuse
  • C. Recycle
  • D. Overexploit
Answer: D. Overexploit

Explanation: Resource conservation promotes reduce, reuse, and recycle. Overexploitation is the opposite of conservation.
