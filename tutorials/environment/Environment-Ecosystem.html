Environment & Ecosystem

Complete tutorial: Environment meaning & components, Biotic & Abiotic Components, Ecosystem & its Structure, Food Chain & Food Web, Ecological Pyramids, Energy Flow, Biogeochemical Cycles for UGC NET Paper-1.

Environment — पर्यावरण

Environment वह surrounding है जो living organisms को influence करती है और उनसे interact करती है। इसमें physical, chemical, biological और social factors शामिल हैं।

Environment = Physical + Chemical + Biological + Social Factors

Key Definitions:

  • Environment (Physical): All external conditions affecting an organism — air, water, land, temperature, etc.
  • Environment (Ecological): The sum total of all living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) factors.

Types of Environment:

  • Natural Environment: Forests, rivers, mountains, atmosphere (created by nature)
  • Human-made Environment: Buildings, roads, industries (modified by humans)
  • Social Environment: Culture, traditions, economic conditions
2

Components of Environment — पर्यावरण के घटक

  • Physical Environment — भौतिक पर्यावरण:
    • Lithosphere (land/earth)
    • Hydrosphere (water bodies)
    • Atmosphere (air/gases)
  • Biological Environment — जैविक पर्यावरण:
    • Plants (flora)
    • Animals (fauna)
    • Microorganisms
  • Social/Cultural Environment — सामाजिक पर्यावरण:
    • Population, culture, economy, technology, institutions
3

Biotic & Abiotic Components — जैविक एवं अजैविक घटक

  • Biotic Components — जैविक घटक (Living): All living organisms in an ecosystem.
    • Producers (Autotrophs): Plants, algae, cyanobacteria — make their own food
    • Consumers (Heterotrophs): Animals — depend on others for food
      • Primary Consumers: Herbivores (deer, rabbit)
      • Secondary Consumers: Carnivores (fox, frog)
      • Tertiary Consumers: Top carnivores (lion, eagle)
    • Decomposers (Saprotrophs): Bacteria, fungi — break down dead organic matter
  • Abiotic Components — अजैविक घटक (Non-living):
    • Physical: Sunlight, temperature, rainfall, wind, water, soil, topography
    • Chemical: Oxygen, carbon dioxide, nitrogen, minerals, pH, salinity
4

Ecosystem — पारिस्थितिकी तंत्र

Ecosystem living organisms (biotic) और non-living environment (abiotic) का functional unit है जो energy flow और nutrient cycling के माध्यम से interact करते हैं।

Ecosystem = Biotic Community + Abiotic Environment + Interaction + Energy Flow

Key Points:

  • Coined by A.G. Tansley (1935)
  • Ecosystem = Community + Environment
  • Ecosystems can be natural (forest, pond) or artificial (crop field, aquarium)

Types of Ecosystems:

  • Terrestrial: Forest, grassland, desert, tundra
  • Aquatic: Freshwater (pond, river, lake), Marine (ocean, sea), Estuarine
5

Structure of Ecosystem — पारिस्थितिकी तंत्र की संरचना

  • Biotic Structure — जैविक संरचना:
    • Producers (Autotrophs): Primary producers — plants, algae
    • Consumers (Heterotrophs): Primary, Secondary, Tertiary
    • Decomposers: Bacteria, fungi
  • Abiotic Structure — अजैविक संरचना:
    • Physical factors: Sunlight, temperature, humidity, water, soil
    • Chemical factors: Oxygen, CO₂, nitrogen, minerals
  • Functional Aspects — कार्यात्मक पहलू:
    • Energy Flow: Sun → Producers → Consumers → Decomposers
    • Nutrient Cycling: Biogeochemical cycles (C, N, O, P, Water)
    • Food Chain & Food Web: Feeding relationships
6

Food Chain — खाद्य श्रृंखला

Food Chain organisms का एक linear sequence है जिसमें energy और nutrients एक organism से दूसरे में transfer होते हैं — जब एक organism दूसरे को eats करता है।

Food Chain = Sun → Producer → Primary Consumer → Secondary Consumer → Tertiary Consumer → Decomposer

Example:

  • Grassland: Sun → Grass → Deer → Lion
  • Pond: Sun → Algae → Fish → Heron
  • Forest: Sun → Tree → Caterpillar → Bird → Eagle

Trophic Levels — पोषण स्तर:

  • T₁ (Producers): Plants, algae
  • T₂ (Primary Consumers): Herbivores
  • T₃ (Secondary Consumers): Carnivores eating herbivores
  • T₄ (Tertiary Consumers): Top carnivores
  • Decomposers: Bacteria, fungi
7

Food Web — खाद्य जाल

Food Web interconnected food chains का network है — एक organism कई organisms को eat कर सकता है और कई organisms द्वारा eat किया जा सकता है।

Food Web = Multiple Interconnected Food Chains

Key Points:

  • More complex and realistic than food chain
  • Shows multiple feeding relationships
  • Provides stability to the ecosystem
  • If one species is removed, others can still survive

Example:

  • Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle
  • Grass → Rabbit → Fox
  • Seeds → Mouse → Snake → Hawk
  • These interconnected chains form a food web
8

Food Chain vs Food Web — खाद्य श्रृंखला vs खाद्य जाल

BasisFood ChainFood Web
NatureLinear, single pathComplex, multiple paths
OrganismsOne organism per trophic levelMultiple organisms per trophic level
StabilityLess stableMore stable
InteractionsSimpleComplex
Energy FlowOne directionMultiple directions
9

Ecological Pyramids — पारिस्थितिक पिरामिड

Ecological Pyramids trophic levels में organisms के number, biomass या energy को graphically represent करते हैं।

Ecological Pyramid = Trophic Levels + Number/Biomass/Energy

Types:

  • Pyramid of Numbers — संख्या पिरामिड: Number of individuals at each trophic level
  • Pyramid of Biomass — बायोमास पिरामिड: Total dry weight of organisms at each trophic level
  • Pyramid of Energy — ऊर्जा पिरामिड: Amount of energy at each trophic level

Key Point: Energy pyramid is always upright (10% law — only 10% energy transferred to next level).

10

Types of Pyramids — पिरामिड के प्रकार

  • Pyramid of Numbers:
    • Upright: Grassland ecosystem (grass → rabbit → fox)
    • Inverted: Tree ecosystem (one tree → many insects → many birds)
  • Pyramid of Biomass:
    • Upright: Most terrestrial ecosystems (plants have max biomass)
    • Inverted: Aquatic ecosystems (phytoplankton → zooplankton → fish — phytoplankton biomass less but more productive)
  • Pyramid of Energy:
    • Always upright (energy decreases at each trophic level due to 10% law)
10% Law: Only 10% of energy from one trophic level is transferred to the next (Lindeman)
11

Energy Flow — ऊर्जा प्रवाह

Energy Flow ecosystem में energy का one-way movement है — sun से producers तक, फिर consumers और decomposers तक।

Sun → Producers → Consumers → Decomposers (One-way flow)

Key Points:

  • Energy enters through photosynthesis (sun → producers)
  • Energy flows in a single direction (not recycled)
  • 10% Law (Lindeman): Only 10% of energy passes to next trophic level
  • 90% energy is lost as heat (respiration, metabolism, etc.)

Energy Flow vs Nutrient Cycling:

  • Energy Flow: One-way, not recycled
  • Nutrient Cycling: Cyclic, recycled (C, N, O, P, Water)
12

Biogeochemical Cycles — जैव-भू-रासायनिक चक्र

Biogeochemical Cycles nutrients का movement and recycling है — between biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) components of the ecosystem.

Biogeochemical Cycle = Biological + Geological + Chemical = Nutrient Recycling

Types:

  • Gaseous Cycles — गैसीय चक्र: Carbon, Nitrogen, Oxygen, Water
  • Sedimentary Cycles — तलछटी चक्र: Phosphorus, Sulphur, Calcium
13

Carbon Cycle — कार्बन चक्र

Carbon Cycle carbon के atmosphere, organisms, oceans, soil, rocks के बीच movement को दर्शाता है।

  • Sources of CO₂: Respiration, decomposition, combustion (fossil fuels, forests), volcanic activity
  • Sinks (Removal): Photosynthesis (plants absorb CO₂), oceans (dissolve CO₂), fossil fuel formation
  • Human Impact: Burning fossil fuels increases atmospheric CO₂ → global warming
14

Nitrogen Cycle — नाइट्रोजन चक्र

Nitrogen Cycle nitrogen का atmosphere, soil, organisms के बीच conversion और movement है।

  • Nitrogen Fixation — नाइट्रोजन स्थिरीकरण: Atmospheric N₂ → ammonia (NH₃) by bacteria (Rhizobium), lightning, industrial
  • Nitrification — नाइट्रीकरण: Ammonia (NH₃) → Nitrites (NO₂⁻) → Nitrates (NO₃⁻) (by bacteria: Nitrosomonas, Nitrobacter)
  • Assimilation — आत्मसात: Plants absorb nitrates; animals eat plants
  • Ammonification — अमोनीकरण: Dead organisms → ammonia (NH₃) (by decomposers)
  • Denitrification — विनाइट्रीकरण: Nitrates → N₂ gas (by bacteria: Pseudomonas)
15

Water Cycle — जल चक्र

Water Cycle (Hydrological Cycle) water का atmosphere, land, oceans के बीच continuous movement है।

  • Evaporation — वाष्पीकरण: Water changes from liquid to vapour (oceans, lakes, rivers)
  • Transpiration — वाष्पोत्सर्जन: Water loss from plants through leaves
  • Condensation — संघनन: Water vapour → clouds (liquid droplets)
  • Precipitation — वर्षण: Rain, snow, hail (water returns to earth)
  • Runoff — अपवाह: Water flows over land surface
  • Infiltration — अंतःस्रावण: Water seeps into soil/groundwater
16

Oxygen Cycle — ऑक्सीजन चक्र

Oxygen Cycle oxygen का atmosphere, organisms, water के बीच movement है।

  • Photosynthesis: Plants release O₂ (6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂)
  • Respiration: Organisms consume O₂ and release CO₂
  • Decomposition: Decomposers use O₂ during decomposition
  • Combustion: Burning of fossil fuels uses O₂
17

Common Exam Traps — सामान्य परीक्षा जाल

  • Trap 1: Ecosystem = only biotic components → ❌ Ecosystem = Biotic + Abiotic + Interaction
  • Trap 2: Food chain = food web → ❌ Food chain is linear; food web is complex network
  • Trap 3: Energy pyramid can be inverted → ❌ Energy pyramid is always upright (10% law)
  • Trap 4: Energy is recycled in ecosystem → ❌ Energy flows one-way; nutrients are recycled
  • Trap 5: Carbon cycle = only photosynthesis → ❌ Includes respiration, decomposition, combustion, etc.
  • Trap 6: Nitrogen fixation = only industrial → ❌ Biological fixation (Rhizobium) is major
  • Trap 7: Pyramid of numbers is always upright → ❌ Can be inverted in tree ecosystems
18

Practice Questions — अभ्यास प्रश्न

Question 1

Which of the following is an abiotic component of an ecosystem?

  • A. Plants
  • B. Animals
  • C. Bacteria
  • D. Sunlight
Answer: D. Sunlight

Explanation: Sunlight is a non-living (abiotic) component. Plants, animals, and bacteria are biotic components.

Question 2

In a food chain, grass → deer → lion, the deer is a:

  • A. Producer
  • B. Primary consumer
  • C. Secondary consumer
  • D. Decomposer
Answer: B. Primary consumer

Explanation: Deer is a herbivore (primary consumer) eating grass (producer). Lion is the secondary/tertiary consumer.

Question 3

The pyramid of energy in an ecosystem is always:

  • A. Inverted
  • B. Upright
  • C. Spindle-shaped
  • D. Irregular
Answer: B. Upright

Explanation: The pyramid of energy is always upright because energy decreases at each trophic level (10% law).

Question 4

Which bacteria is responsible for nitrogen fixation in root nodules of legumes?

  • A. Nitrosomonas
  • B. Rhizobium
  • C. Nitrobacter
  • D. Pseudomonas
Answer: B. Rhizobium

Explanation: Rhizobium is the nitrogen-fixing bacteria found in root nodules of legumes. Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter are nitrifying bacteria; Pseudomonas is denitrifying.

Question 5

Which of the following is NOT a biogeochemical cycle?

  • A. Carbon cycle
  • B. Nitrogen cycle
  • C. Energy cycle
  • D. Water cycle
Answer: C. Energy cycle

Explanation: Energy flows one-way and is not recycled. Carbon, Nitrogen, and Water are biogeochemical cycles where nutrients are recycled.

Question 6

The term 'ecosystem' was coined by:

  • A. Charles Darwin
  • B. A.G. Tansley
  • C. E.P. Odum
  • D. John Muir
Answer: B. A.G. Tansley

Explanation: A.G. Tansley coined the term 'ecosystem' in 1935.

One-Page Revision — एक पेज में पुनरावृत्ति

Environment = Physical + Biological + Social
Ecosystem = Biotic + Abiotic + Interaction + Energy Flow

Biotic Components:

  • Producers (Autotrophs), Consumers (Heterotrophs), Decomposers (Saprotrophs)

Abiotic Components:

  • Physical: Sunlight, temperature, water, soil
  • Chemical: O₂, CO₂, N₂, minerals

Food Chain & Food Web:

  • Food Chain = Linear (single path)
  • Food Web = Network (multiple paths)

Ecological Pyramids:

  • Numbers (can be upright/inverted)
  • Biomass (upright in terrestrial; inverted in aquatic)
  • Energy (always upright — 10% law)

Energy Flow:

  • One-way (Sun → Producers → Consumers → Decomposers)
  • 10% Law: Only 10% energy transferred to next trophic level

Biogeochemical Cycles:

  • Gaseous: Carbon, Nitrogen, Oxygen, Water
  • Sedimentary: Phosphorus, Sulphur

Exam Formula:

Ecosystem = Structure + Function = (Biotic + Abiotic) + (Energy Flow + Nutrient Cycling)
