Environmental Laws, Policies & International Agreements

Complete tutorial: Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, Water Act, 1974, Air Act, 1981, Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, Forest Conservation Act, 1980, Biological Diversity Act, 2002, National Green Tribunal, NAPCC, Stockholm Conference, Montreal Protocol, Rio Earth Summit, Kyoto Protocol, Paris Agreement, International Solar Alliance, MDGs & SDGs.

Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 — पर्यावरण (संरक्षण) अधिनियम, 1986

Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 India का एक umbrella legislation है — Bhopal Gas Tragedy के बाद enacted।

  • Purpose: Protection and improvement of the environment
  • Key Provisions: Central government can take measures to protect environment; standards for emissions/effluents; regulation of hazardous substances
  • Significance: Provides framework for environmental protection in India
  • Amendments: Includes provisions for environmental impact assessment (EIA)
2

Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 — जल अधिनियम, 1974

Water Act, 1974 India's first comprehensive law for water pollution control.

  • Purpose: Prevention and control of water pollution; maintaining or restoring water quality
  • Key Provisions: Establishment of Central and State Pollution Control Boards; consent mechanism for industries discharging effluents
  • Amendments: 1988 amendments strengthened provisions
  • Significance: First pollution control legislation in India
3

Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 — वायु अधिनियम, 1981

Air Act, 1981 was enacted to control air pollution in India (based on the Stockholm Conference recommendations).

  • Purpose: Prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution
  • Key Provisions: Standards for emissions; consent mechanism for industries; monitoring of air quality
  • Significance: Provides legal framework for air quality management
4

Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 — वन्य जीव संरक्षण अधिनियम, 1972

Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 provides legal protection to wild animals and birds.

  • Purpose: Protection of wild animals, birds, and plants
  • Key Provisions: Establishment of protected areas (National Parks, Sanctuaries); prohibition of hunting; trade restrictions; schedules for protected species
  • Schedules: Schedule I (highest protection — tigers, elephants, etc.), Schedule II-V (varying protection)
  • Project Tiger: Launched in 1973 under this Act
5

Forest Conservation Act, 1980 — वन संरक्षण अधिनियम, 1980

Forest Conservation Act, 1980 restricts the conversion of forest land for non-forest purposes.

  • Purpose: Conservation of forests and regulation of deforestation
  • Key Provisions: Prior approval of Central Government required for de-reservation of forests, diversion of forest land
  • Significance: Has helped control deforestation; important for forest conservation
  • 2005 Amendment: Strengthened provisions and penalties
6

Biological Diversity Act, 2002 — जैव विविधता अधिनियम, 2002

Biological Diversity Act, 2002 implements the CBD (Convention on Biological Diversity) in India.

  • Purpose: Conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use, and equitable sharing of benefits
  • Key Provisions: Regulation of access to biological resources; benefit-sharing with local communities; establishment of National Biodiversity Authority (NBA), State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs), Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)
  • Significance: Protects India's biodiversity and indigenous knowledge
7

National Green Tribunal (NGT) — राष्ट्रीय हरित अधिकरण

National Green Tribunal (NGT) is a specialised judicial body for environmental disputes in India.

  • Established: 2010 under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010
  • Purpose: Speedy disposal of environment-related cases
  • Jurisdiction: Civil cases relating to environmental protection and conservation; issues concerning forest and biodiversity
  • Key Principle: Applies the Precautionary Principle and Polluter Pays Principle
  • Benches: Principal Bench in Delhi; Regional Benches in various cities
8

National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC) — राष्ट्रीय जलवायु परिवर्तन कार्य योजना

NAPCC is India's comprehensive climate change action plan — launched in 2008.

  • Purpose: Address climate change through eight national missions
  • Eight Missions:
    • 1. National Solar Mission
    • 2. National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency
    • 3. National Mission on Sustainable Habitat
    • 4. National Water Mission
    • 5. National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem
    • 6. National Mission for a Green India
    • 7. National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture
    • 8. National Mission on Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change
  • Significance: India's framework for climate action; focuses on both mitigation and adaptation
9

Stockholm Conference, 1972 — स्टॉकहोम सम्मेलन, 1972

United Nations Conference on the Human Environment (Stockholm Conference), 1972 was the first major international conference on environmental issues.

  • Also known as: Stockholm Conference on the Human Environment
  • Date: June 5-16, 1972
  • Key Outcomes:
    • Declaration on the Human Environment (Stockholm Declaration) — 26 principles
    • Establishment of UNEP (United Nations Environment Programme)
    • June 5 declared as World Environment Day
  • Significance: Marked the beginning of international environmental governance
10

Montreal Protocol, 1987 — मॉन्ट्रियल प्रोटोकॉल, 1987

Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer, 1987 is an international treaty to protect the ozone layer.

  • Purpose: Phase out ozone-depleting substances (ODS) — CFCs, halons, etc.
  • Date: Signed in 1987, entered into force in 1989
  • Key Features:
    • Legally binding phase-out schedule for developed and developing countries
    • Multilateral Fund to assist developing countries
    • Kigali Amendment (2016) — phase down HFCs (hydrofluorocarbons)
  • Significance: Most successful environmental treaty; ozone layer is recovering
11

Rio Earth Summit, 1992 — रियो पृथ्वी शिखर सम्मेलन, 1992

United Nations Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED), 1992 — also known as the Rio Earth Summit.

  • Also known as: Rio Earth Summit (1992)
  • Date: June 3-14, 1992
  • Key Outcomes:
    • Agenda 21: Comprehensive plan of action for sustainable development
    • Rio Declaration: 27 principles on environment and development
    • UNFCCC: Framework Convention on Climate Change
    • CBD: Convention on Biological Diversity
    • Forest Principles: Non-legally binding statement on forests
  • Significance: Major milestone in international environmental governance
12

Kyoto Protocol, 1997 — क्योटो प्रोटोकॉल, 1997

Kyoto Protocol is an international treaty to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

  • Date: Adopted in 1997, entered into force in 2005
  • Purpose: Legally binding emission reduction targets for developed countries (Annex I countries)
  • Key Features:
    • 5% reduction from 1990 levels (for Annex I countries)
    • Flexibility mechanisms: CDM, JI, Emissions Trading
    • Commitment periods: 2008-2012 (first), 2013-2020 (second/Doha Amendment)
  • Significance: First legally binding climate agreement; but the US did not ratify; Canada withdrew
13

Paris Agreement, 2015 — पेरिस समझौता, 2015

Paris Agreement is the current global climate change agreement under UNFCCC.

  • Date: Adopted in December 2015, entered into force in November 2016
  • Purpose: Limit global temperature rise to well below 2°C (and pursue 1.5°C)
  • Key Features:
    • NDCs: Nationally Determined Contributions — voluntary targets by all countries
    • Global Stocktake: Review progress every 5 years
    • Transparency: Reporting and review mechanism
    • Finance: Developed countries to provide climate finance
    • CBDR-RC: Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities
  • Significance: Universal agreement (all countries); NDCs are voluntary (not legally binding targets)
14

International Solar Alliance (ISA) — अंतर्राष्ट्रीय सौर गठबंधन

International Solar Alliance (ISA) is an initiative by India to promote solar energy globally.

  • Launched: 2015 (at COP21 in Paris), jointly by India and France
  • Purpose: Promote solar energy, reduce dependence on fossil fuels
  • Headquarters: India (National Institute of Solar Energy, Gurugram)
  • Membership: Countries between the Tropic of Cancer and Tropic of Capricorn (solar-rich countries)
  • Significance: India's leadership in renewable energy; global cooperation for solar energy
15

MDGs & SDGs — सहस्राब्दि विकास लक्ष्य एवं सतत विकास लक्ष्य

  • MDGs (Millennium Development Goals) — सहस्राब्दि विकास लक्ष्य:
    • Adopted in 2000 (8 goals)
    • Target: 2015
    • Focused on poverty, health, education, gender equality, environmental sustainability
    • Successor: SDGs
  • SDGs (Sustainable Development Goals) — सतत विकास लक्ष्य:
    • Adopted in 2015 (17 goals)
    • Target: 2030
    • Broader than MDGs — includes economic, social, environmental dimensions
    • All 17 goals: No Poverty, Zero Hunger, Good Health, Quality Education, Gender Equality, Clean Water, Clean Energy, Decent Work, Industry & Innovation, Reduced Inequality, Sustainable Cities, Responsible Consumption, Climate Action, Life Below Water, Life on Land, Peace & Justice, Partnerships

Common Exam Traps — सामान्य परीक्षा जाल

  • Trap 1: EPA 1986 was enacted after the Bhopal Gas Tragedy → ✅ Correct.
  • Trap 2: Water Act 1974 came before Air Act 1981 → ✅ Correct.
  • Trap 3: Montreal Protocol = climate change → ❌ Montreal Protocol is for ozone depletion (CFCs).
  • Trap 4: Kyoto Protocol = all countries → ❌ Kyoto Protocol had targets only for developed countries.
  • Trap 5: Paris Agreement = legally binding targets → ❌ NDCs are voluntary (not legally binding).
  • Trap 6: NGT = 1990 → ❌ NGT was established in 2010.
  • Trap 7: Rio Summit = 1972 → ❌ Rio Summit was 1992 (Stockholm was 1972).
  • Trap 8: MDGs = 17 goals → ❌ MDGs = 8 goals; SDGs = 17 goals.

Practice Questions — अभ्यास प्रश्न

Question 1

The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 was enacted after which incident?

  • A. Chernobyl disaster
  • B. Bhopal Gas Tragedy
  • C. Fukushima disaster
  • D. Exxon Valdez oil spill
Answer: B. Bhopal Gas Tragedy

Explanation: The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 was enacted after the Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984).

Question 2

Which protocol is associated with the protection of the ozone layer?

  • A. Kyoto Protocol
  • B. Montreal Protocol
  • C. Paris Agreement
  • D. Cartagena Protocol
Answer: B. Montreal Protocol

Explanation: The Montreal Protocol (1987) is for the protection of the ozone layer by phasing out CFCs.

Question 3

The National Green Tribunal (NGT) was established in:

  • A. 2000
  • B. 2005
  • C. 2010
  • D. 2015
Answer: C. 2010

Explanation: The National Green Tribunal was established in 2010 under the NGT Act, 2010.

Question 4

Which agreement introduced Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)?

  • A. Kyoto Protocol
  • B. Paris Agreement
  • C. Montreal Protocol
  • D. Rio Summit
Answer: B. Paris Agreement

Explanation: The Paris Agreement (2015) introduced NDCs — voluntary climate targets by countries.

Question 5

How many Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are there?

  • A. 8
  • B. 12
  • C. 15
  • D. 17
Answer: D. 17

Explanation: There are 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), adopted in 2015, target 2030.

Question 6

The International Solar Alliance (ISA) was launched jointly by India and:

  • A. USA
  • B. France
  • C. Germany
  • D. UK
Answer: B. France

Explanation: The ISA was launched in 2015 by India and France at COP21 in Paris.
