Concept & Classification of Factors

Factors Affecting Teaching वे personal, academic, psychological, material, environmental और institutional conditions हैं जो teaching-learning process को facilitate या obstruct करती हैं।

Effective Teaching = Teacher Factors + Learner Factors + Support Material + Instructional Facilities + Learning Environment + Institutional Support

Major Classification:

  • A. Teacher-Related: Subject knowledge, pedagogy, communication, experience, attitude, motivation, personality, classroom management, assessment, technology, reflection
  • B. Learner-Related: Prior knowledge, readiness, intelligence, aptitude, interest, motivation, health, attention, language, socio-economic background
  • C. Support-Material: Textbooks, notes, charts, e-content, examples, accessibility, accuracy
  • D. Instructional Facilities: Classroom, library, laboratory, ICT, Internet, smart classroom, assistive technologies
  • E. Learning Environment: Physical, social, psychological, academic, digital
  • F. Institutional: Leadership, academic policies, curriculum, faculty development, workload, funding, student support, quality assurance
2

Teacher-Related Factors

Effective Teacher = Content Knowledge + Pedagogy + Communication + Sensitivity + Assessment + Reflection
  • Subject Knowledge: Facts, concepts, principles, theories, applications, recent developments
  • Pedagogical Knowledge: How to teach, explain, assess, identify difficulties
  • Pedagogical Content Knowledge (PCK): Subject knowledge + best way to represent that subject
  • Communication Skills: Clarity, logical organisation, voice modulation, listening, questioning, examples, bilingual explanation where required
  • Teacher's Attitude: Respect, patience, empathy, fairness, acceptance of diversity, constructive feedback
  • Motivation & Commitment: Lesson preparation, updated knowledge, learner support, professional development
  • Teaching Experience: Anticipating classroom situations, selecting examples, identifying difficulties, managing time
  • Teacher Personality: Emotional stability, confidence, enthusiasm, patience, openness, flexibility, professional ethics
  • Classroom Management: Clear expectations, time management, positive discipline, conflict management, inclusive seating
  • Assessment Skills: Valid questions, formative assessment, feedback, rubric development, misconception identification
  • Use of Technology: Presentation, LMS, online quiz, video, simulation, accessibility tools (purposeful integration)
  • Reflective Ability: Plan → Teach → Observe → Evaluate → Improve
3

Learner-Related Factors

Learner Readiness = Ability + Prior Knowledge + Motivation + Health + Willingness
  • Prior Knowledge: New learning builds on existing knowledge
  • Readiness & Maturation: Physically, cognitively, emotionally ready
  • Intelligence: Reasoning, learning, adaptation potential (but achievement ≠ intelligence alone)
  • Aptitude: Specific area future learning potential
  • Interest: Relevance, engagement, attention
  • Motivation: Intrinsic (curiosity, mastery) + Extrinsic (marks, certificate, promotion)
  • Aspiration Level: Realistic challenging goals → motivation & progress
  • Attention & Concentration: Affected by interest, fatigue, anxiety, noise, sleep, task difficulty, digital distraction
  • Physical & Mental Health: Nutrition, sleep, illness, stress, anxiety, fatigue
  • Language Proficiency: Medium of instruction familiarity affects comprehension and expression
  • Learning Pace: Flexible practice, remedial support, enrichment, differentiated tasks
  • Personality & Self-Concept: Confidence, persistence, self-efficacy, emotional stability
  • Socio-Economic & Cultural Background: Resources, digital access, language exposure, family support, expectations
4

Support Material Factors

Good Support Material = Accurate + Relevant + Organised + Accessible + Engaging
  • Accuracy: Factually and conceptually correct; outdated statistics and incorrect definitions create misconceptions
  • Relevance: Related to learning objectives and learner needs
  • Organisation: Clear headings, logical sequence, examples, summary, practice questions, visual hierarchy
  • Language & Readability: Simple but academic language, appropriate font size, clear terminology, bilingual support, short paragraphs
  • Learner Appropriateness: Age, prior knowledge, language, cognitive level, cultural background, special needs
  • Interactivity: Answer, apply, compare, reflect, practise, create opportunities
  • Accessibility: Alternative text, captions, screen-reader compatibility, readable typography, colour contrast, keyboard navigation, audio alternative
  • Availability: Timely availability of textbooks, reading materials, digital content
5

Instructional Facilities

  • Classroom Size & Seating: Overcrowding reduces interaction, individual feedback, visibility; increases noise; flexible seating useful for group discussion
  • Library: Updated books, journals, databases, reading space, digital access, research support, trained staff
  • Laboratory: Functional equipment, safety, consumables, demonstration, practical time, technical assistance
  • ICT Infrastructure: Reliable Internet, devices, projector, LMS, technical support, cybersecurity, backup power, accessible software
  • Maintenance & Technical Support: Equipment must be functional; technical support essential for actual instructional support
6

Learning Environment

Learning Environment = Physical + Social + Psychological + Academic + Digital Conditions
7

Physical Environment

  • Light, ventilation, temperature, noise, cleanliness, space, seating, visibility, acoustics, safety
  • Poor lighting → eyestrain; Noise → reduced concentration; Poor ventilation → fatigue; Extreme temperature → discomfort; Overcrowding → low interaction
8

Social Environment

  • Teacher–student relationship, peer interaction, cooperation, respect, inclusion, communication, group belongingness
  • Positive social environment encourages learners to ask questions and share ideas
9

Psychological/Emotional Environment

  • Mistakes are part of learning; asking questions is safe; no public humiliation; diversity respected; constructive feedback
Psychological Safety → Participation → Feedback → Better Learning
10

Academic Environment

  • High but realistic expectations, academic honesty, inquiry, discussion, research culture, feedback, collaboration, intellectual freedom

Exam Trap: High expectations and unreasonable pressure are not the same.

11

Positive Reinforcement

Praise, encouragement and recognition support learner motivation.

  • Better Feedback: "You have made a clear comparison of both theories; adding evidence will make your answer stronger."
  • Weak Feedback: "Good."
12

Negative Learning Environment

  • Fear, discrimination, favouritism, bullying, excessive competition, public humiliation, rigid control, lack of discussion, unsafe infrastructure, unclear expectations
  • Impact: Anxiety, low participation, absenteeism, surface learning, low motivation, dropout, reduced creativity
13

Digital Learning Environment

  • LMS usability, device access, connectivity, online communication, digital content, data privacy, cyber safety, technical support, accessibility

Exam Point: Online learning may have physical distance, but social presence and teacher support must be maintained.

14

Institutional Factors

Supportive Institution = Academic Leadership + Resources + Autonomy + Development + Accountability
  • Institutional Leadership: Clear academic vision, teacher support, collaboration, resource allocation, innovation, fair decision-making
  • Curriculum: Relevant, updated, flexible, outcome-oriented, inclusive, skill-linked, research-informed
  • Teacher Workload: Excessive teaching load, administrative work, documentation reduce lesson preparation, feedback and research time
  • Faculty Development: Orientation, refresher programmes, workshops, research support, technology training, mentoring, peer observation, professional learning communities
  • Academic Autonomy: Freedom to select teaching method, use contextual examples, innovative assessment, suggest course improvement (with accountability)
  • Student Support Services: Academic counselling, mentoring, career guidance, psychological counselling, remedial support, scholarship, disability support, grievance redressal
  • Funding & Resource Allocation: Affects faculty recruitment, library resources, laboratory, technology, maintenance, student support, research
  • Faculty Availability: Teacher shortage → large classes, high workload, limited feedback, course cancellation, reduced mentoring
  • Institutional Culture: Positive: teamwork, mutual respect, academic integrity, research, innovation, resource sharing; Negative: internal conflict, favouritism, fear, rigid bureaucracy, resistance to change
  • Quality Assurance: Learner feedback, peer review, course-outcome analysis, assessment audit, curriculum review, faculty development, academic support (aim: continuous improvement, not punishment)
15

Interaction among Different Factors

Factors do not work independently; they interact with each other.

  • Example 1: Teacher is good but learners have weak prior knowledge → diagnostic assessment and prerequisite teaching needed
  • Example 2: Digital content is good but Internet is unavailable → material is practically inaccessible
  • Example 3: Facilities are excellent but environment is humiliating → participation and learning decrease
  • Example 4: Learners are motivated but curriculum is outdated → engagement and career relevance affected
A weakness in one factor can reduce the strength of another factor.

Important Exam Points

  • Teaching effectiveness is a multi-factor phenomenon
  • Subject knowledge is necessary but not sufficient alone
  • Teacher's explanation ability is more direct than qualification
  • Learner motivation, aptitude and interest are influential factors
  • Prior experience is an important learner characteristic
  • Mental health, attention, readiness and fatigue affect learning
  • Learning material must be accurate, relevant, readable and accessible
  • Non-availability of reading materials reduces instructional effectiveness
  • Smart class availability is an instructional facility factor
  • Teacher praise creates a positive learning environment
  • Family and socio-cultural diversity are environmental factors
  • Overcrowding affects physical and instructional environment
  • Institutional leadership and faculty development affect teaching quality
  • ICT is not a complete replacement for the teacher
  • Learning autonomy support increases motivation
  • Public humiliation damages psychological safety
  • Technology availability and technology integration are different
  • Teaching methods should be diversified according to individual differences
  • Positive environment encourages open discussion and sharing
  • Effective institution supports teamwork and academic development

Motivation is only internal.Both internal and external motivation are possible.

Verified PYQs

PYQ 1: UGC NET, 12 Nov 2020, Shift 1

Question: Which factors are related to instructional facilities and learning environment?
Answer: Non-availability of textbooks/reading materials, proactive moves in interactive processes, availability of smart classes.

Explanation: Textbooks → support material/instructional environment; Proactive interaction → learning process/environment; Smart classes → institutional infrastructure.

PYQ 2: UGC NET, 18 Dec 2018, Shift 2

Question: Learner characteristics helpful in ensuring teaching effectiveness?
Answer: Motivation, prior experience, interpersonal relations.

Formula: Effective Learner Participation = Motivation + Prior Experience + Interpersonal Relations

PYQ 3: UGC NET, 4 Dec 2021, Shift 1

Question: Environmental factors influencing student learning?
Answer: Family, motivation, socio-cultural diversities.

PYQ 4: UGC NET, 24 Nov 2021, Shift 2

Question: Most influential factors of learning?
Answer: Motivation, aptitude, interest.

Formula: Learning Engagement = Motivation + Aptitude + Interest

PYQ 5: UGC NET, 14 March 2023, Shift 2

Question: Factors that affect learning?
Answer: Mental health, attention, readiness, fatigue.

PYQ 6: UGC NET, 21 Oct 2022, Shift 2

Question: Effectiveness of teaching depends on?
Answer: Ability of the teacher to explain concepts to learners.

Formula: Subject Knowledge + Explanation Ability = Classroom Effectiveness

PYQ 7: UGC NET, 4 Dec 2019, Shift 2

Question: Factors that do NOT contribute to teaching effectiveness?
Answer: Socio-economic background of teacher, teacher's skill in pleasing students, teacher's personal contact with students (if beyond professional boundaries). Subject knowledge DOES contribute.

PYQ 8: UGC NET, 9 Oct 2020, Shift 1

Question: Instructional technology can replace the teacher when personality development is the objective?
Answer: FALSE. Technology can assist, but cannot completely replace the teacher, especially in personality development (human interaction, empathy, mentoring, social experience, value-oriented guidance).
