Important Educational Thinkers: Dewey, Vygotsky & Skinner

Complete tutorial: John Dewey (Pragmatism, Learning by Doing, Reflective Thinking), Lev Vygotsky (Social Constructivism, ZPD, Scaffolding), B.F. Skinner (Operant Conditioning, Reinforcement, Programmed Instruction).

Master Association

John Dewey → Pragmatism → Learning by Doing → Reflective Thinking
Lev Vygotsky → Social Constructivism → ZPD → Scaffolding
B. F. Skinner → Behaviourism → Operant Conditioning → Reinforcement
2

John Dewey — Introduction

John Dewey (1859–1952) — American philosopher, psychologist and educational thinker.

  • Pragmatism
  • Instrumentalism
  • Progressive Education
  • Learning by Doing
  • Experience-based Learning
  • Problem-solving
  • Reflective Thinking
  • Democratic Education
Dewey → Pragmatism → Experience → Activity → Problem-Solving → Reflection
3

Dewey's Concept of Education

Education is not merely preparation for future life. Education is life itself and a continuous process of growth.

Education is a continuous reconstruction of experience.
Dewey = Experience + Inquiry + Reflection + Action
4

Learning by Doing

Learner learns by actively participating in meaningful activities: observe, question, experiment, problem-solve, see results, reflect on experience.

Example: Teacher doesn't give definition of "Environmental Pollution" but has students survey campus waste — identify sources, collect data, analyse causes, propose solutions, reflect on project.

Exam Trap: Physical activity alone is NOT Learning by Doing. Activity must be purposeful, meaningful, connected with objectives, and followed by reflection.

5

Experience and Education

  • Continuity of Experience: Present experience affects future learning and development.
  • Interaction: Learning occurs from the interaction of learner's internal conditions and external environment.
Learning = Learner × Environment
6

Reflective Thinking

Carefully examining a belief or proposed knowledge based on evidence and consequences.

Problem → Observation/Data → Hypothesis → Reasoning → Testing → Conclusion
  • Felt Difficulty: Learner experiences a problem/doubt
  • Problem Identification: Clearly define the problem
  • Hypothesis: Develop possible solutions
  • Reasoning: Analyse implications of solutions
  • Testing: Test hypothesis with evidence and experience
  • Conclusion: Accept the most appropriate solution
7

Dewey's Democratic Education

Classroom and school as a miniature democratic society.

  • Teacher Role: Designs learning experiences, presents problems, facilitates inquiry, provides resources, does not impose ready-made answers, encourages reflection.
  • Learner Role: Active participant, investigator, problem-solver, collaborator, reflective thinker.
8

Classroom Applications (Dewey)

  • Project Method
  • Problem-solving
  • Laboratory activity
  • Field visit
  • Case study
  • Group investigation
  • Community survey
  • Experiential learning
  • Reflective journal
  • Democratic discussion
Dewey → Philosophical Foundation of Project-Based Learning | William H. Kilpatrick → Project Method
9

Exam Traps: Dewey

  • Dewey → Pragmatism (NOT Idealism)
  • Learning by Doing is NOT merely mechanical activity
  • Experience must be accompanied by reflection
  • Dewey does NOT remove the teacher — teacher is experience designer and facilitator
  • Dewey emphasizes cooperation and democracy over competition
  • Project Method direct association → Kilpatrick; philosophical foundation → Dewey
10

Lev Vygotsky — Introduction

Lev Vygotsky (1896–1934) — Russian psychologist.

Vygotsky → Social Constructivism → Social Interaction → Language → ZPD → Scaffolding
Learning = Social Interaction + Language + Cultural Tools + Guided Participation
11

Social Constructivism

Learner constructs knowledge through interaction with teacher, peers, parents, experts, culture and language.

Learning first occurs at the social level and later becomes internalised at the individual level.
12

Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)

The difference between what a learner can do independently and what they can do with guidance.

ZPD = Potential Performance with Guidance − Independent Performance
  • What learner can do independently: Actual development level
  • What learner can do with support: ZONE OF PROXIMAL DEVELOPMENT
  • What learner cannot yet do even with support: Beyond current reach
13

ZPD Example

Learner can calculate percentage independently but not weighted average. Teacher gives formula hint, shows solved example, explains steps, provides guided practice. With assistance, learner calculates SGPA — this task is in learner's ZPD. After practice, learner independently calculates SGPA — performance has been internalised.

14

More Knowledgeable Other (MKO)

Person or source with more knowledge or competence than the learner.

  • Teacher
  • Parent
  • Skilled peer
  • Expert
  • Mentor
  • Digital tutor
  • Learning resource

Exam Trap: MKO is NOT always a teacher. A competent peer can also be an MKO.

15

Scaffolding

Temporary, structured support to help a learner complete a task.

  • Hints, prompts, guided questions, worked examples, partial solution, visual cues, peer assistance, feedback, task simplification
Scaffolding = Temporary Support + Gradual Withdrawal
Model → Guide → Practise Together → Reduce Support → Independent Performance

Historical Exam Point: Scaffolding concept is strongly related to Vygotsky's ZPD, but the term "scaffolding" is associated with Wood, Bruner and Ross.

16

Language and Thought

  • Social Speech: Communication with others
  • Private Speech: Talking to oneself to guide behaviour and problem-solving (NOT immaturity)
  • Inner Speech: Private speech becomes silent internal thinking
Social Speech → Private Speech → Inner Speech
17

Classroom Applications (Vygotsky)

  • Cooperative learning
  • Peer tutoring
  • Collaborative problem-solving
  • Guided practice
  • Reciprocal teaching
  • Think–Pair–Share
  • Teacher questioning
  • Group project
  • Dialogic teaching
  • Gradual release of responsibility
18

19

Exam Traps: Vygotsky

  • ZPD is NOT independent performance — it's the zone of assisted potential
  • Too easy task → NOT in ZPD
  • Impossible task → NOT in ZPD
  • Scaffolding is NOT permanent support
  • MKO is NOT only the teacher
  • Private speech is NOT immaturity — it's a tool for self-regulation
  • Vygotsky emphasizes social and cultural context
20

B.F. Skinner — Introduction

Burrhus Frederic Skinner (1904–1990) — American behaviourist psychologist.

Skinner → Behaviourism → Operant Conditioning → Reinforcement → Programmed Instruction
Behaviour → Consequence → Future Behaviour
21

Operant Conditioning

Behaviour that operates on the environment, and its consequences affect future behaviour.

Example: Student submits assignments regularly. Teacher gives timely praise and feedback. Assignment submission increases — this is reinforcement.

22

Reinforcement

Reinforcement always aims to increase behaviour.
  • Positive Reinforcement: Add pleasant stimulus → behaviour increases (praise, certificate, recognition)
  • Negative Reinforcement: Remove unpleasant condition → behaviour increases (teacher removes extra remedial drill after work completion)

Exam Trap: Negative reinforcement is NOT punishment. "Negative" means removing a stimulus; it does NOT mean undesirable or harmful.

23

Punishment

Punishment aims to decrease behaviour.
  • Positive Punishment: Add unpleasant consequence → behaviour decreases (formal warning for disruption)
  • Negative Punishment: Remove pleasant privilege → behaviour decreases (remove activity privilege due to misuse)

Ethical Point: Educational punishment should NOT be humiliating, physically harmful, discriminatory, or excessive. Positive discipline and restorative approaches are more appropriate.

24

Reinforcement vs Punishment

ConceptWhat happens?Effect on behaviour
Positive ReinforcementPleasant stimulus addedBehaviour increases
Negative ReinforcementUnpleasant stimulus removedBehaviour increases
Positive PunishmentUnpleasant consequence addedBehaviour decreases
Negative PunishmentPleasant privilege removedBehaviour decreases
Reinforcement increases behaviour. Punishment decreases behaviour. Positive adds; Negative removes.
25

Types of Reinforcers

  • Primary Reinforcer: Biological needs — food, rest, comfort
  • Secondary/Conditioned Reinforcer: Learned value — marks, praise, certificate, token, grade, recognition
26

Reinforcement Schedules

  • Continuous: Every correct response reinforced (good for new behaviour)
  • Fixed Ratio: After fixed number of responses
  • Variable Ratio: After unpredictable number of responses (highly persistent)
  • Fixed Interval: After fixed time interval
  • Variable Interval: After unpredictable time intervals
Ratio = Number of Responses | Interval = Time | Fixed = Predictable | Variable = Unpredictable
27

Shaping

Gradually developing complex behaviour by reinforcing successive approximations.

Example (Research writing): Reinforce correct research problem → then objectives → then literature review → then complete proposal.

Shaping = Reinforcement of Successive Approximations
28

Extinction

When previously reinforced behaviour no longer receives reinforcement, the behaviour gradually decreases.

Exam Trap: Extinction is NOT punishment — reinforcement is withdrawn.

29

Programmed Instruction

Small Step → Active Response → Immediate Feedback → Reinforcement → Next Step
  • Linear Programming: All learners follow the same sequence — associated with Skinner
  • Branching Programming: Response determines different paths — associated with Norman Crowder
Skinner → Linear Programming | Crowder → Branching Programming
30

Classroom Applications (Skinner)

  • Immediate feedback
  • Specific praise
  • Token system
  • Drill and practice
  • Behaviour contracts
  • Programmed learning
  • Computer-assisted practice
  • Mastery of small steps
  • Reinforcing desired participation
  • Shaping complex skills

Better reinforcement: "You have supported your conclusion with relevant evidence." (Specific feedback helps learner understand desired behaviour.)

31

Limitations of Skinner's Approach

  • Excessive emphasis on observable behaviour
  • Limited explanation of internal thought and emotion
  • Risk of over-dependence on external rewards
  • Risk of neglecting creativity and independent inquiry
  • Risk of reducing complex learning to small responses
  • Motivation may decrease when rewards are removed

Balanced Approach: Reinforcement is useful for basic habits and skills, but higher-order learning also requires meaning, inquiry, reflection, collaboration and internal motivation.

Important Exam Traps

  • Dewey → Pragmatism; Vygotsky → Social Constructivism; Skinner → Behaviourism
  • Learning by Doing is NOT just activity — reflection is required
  • ZPD is the area between actual and potential development
  • Scaffolding is temporary
  • MKO can be teacher, peer, or expert
  • Scaffolding term → Wood, Bruner and Ross
  • Negative reinforcement is NOT punishment
  • Reinforcement increases behaviour; punishment decreases behaviour
  • Skinner → Linear Programming; Crowder → Branching Programming
  • Pavlov → Stimulus; Skinner → Consequences; Bandura → Modelling
  • Dewey does NOT remove the teacher — teacher organises experiences
  • Skinner's approach focuses heavily on observable behaviour
35
