Meaning of Evaluation

Evaluation एक systematic process है जिसमें learner के learning outcomes से संबंधित evidence collect किया जाता है, उसकी interpretation की जाती है और निर्धारित objectives या standards के आधार पर value judgement तथा decision लिया जाता है।

Evaluation = Evidence Collection + Interpretation + Value Judgement + Decision
2

ConceptMeaningExample
Measurement — मापनNumerical value to performance40/50 marks
Assessment — आकलनCollect and interpret learning evidenceQuiz, assignment, observation

Examination — परीक्षाFormal test at scheduled timeSemester examination
Testing — परीक्षणMeasure specific ability/achievementAptitude test
Measurement: How much? | Assessment: What and how learned? | Evaluation: How good and what decision?

4

Principles of Good Evaluation

  • Validity — वैधता: Measures what it intends to measure
  • Reliability — विश्वसनीयता: Consistency of results
  • Objectivity — वस्तुनिष्ठता: Minimal scorer bias
  • Practicability — व्यावहारिकता: Economical, easy, time-efficient
  • Comprehensiveness — व्यापकता: Covers cognitive, affective, psychomotor
  • Continuity — निरन्तरता: Throughout the learning process
  • Fairness — निष्पक्षता: Clear, accessible, transparent criteria
  • Diagnostic Value: Identifies errors and learning difficulties
Reliability is necessary but not sufficient for validity.
5

Types of Evaluation

Placement → Diagnostic → Formative → Summative
P–D–F–S = Place → Diagnose → Form/Improve → Sum/Certify
6

Placement Evaluation

Before instruction begins — determines entry behaviour, readiness and appropriate learning level.

  • Determine entry level
  • Know prior knowledge
  • Place in suitable course/group
  • Check prerequisites
  • Determine instructional starting point

Example: Entry test to place learners in Basic/Intermediate/Advanced groups.

7

Diagnostic Evaluation

Identifies specific learning difficulty and its possible cause.

Diagnostic Evaluation = Specific Weakness + Probable Cause
  • Identify difficulty
  • Detailed error analysis
  • Identify cause
  • Remedial teaching
  • Re-assessment
8

Formative Evaluation

During teaching-learning process — monitors progress and provides feedback for improvement.

Formative = During Learning + Feedback + Improvement
  • Identify learning gaps
  • Immediate feedback
  • Modify teaching method
  • Remedial support
  • Learner motivation
9

Summative Evaluation

At the end of course/unit/semester — judges overall achievement.

Summative = End Point + Grading + Certification + Judgement
  • Grading
  • Promotion
  • Certification
  • Selection
  • Overall achievement
10

Formative vs Summative Evaluation

BasisFormativeSummative
TimingDuring learningEnd of learning period
Main purposeImprovementJudgement/certification
FeedbackContinuous and developmentalTerminal/overall
NatureDiagnostic and remedial orientationGrading and certification
StakesGenerally lowGenerally high
Teacher actionModify teachingReport achievement
Assessment conceptAssessment FOR LearningAssessment OF Learning
11

Assessment FOR, AS and OF Learning

  • Assessment FOR Learning: Improve learning — teacher feedback, teaching adjustment — Formative
  • Assessment AS Learning: Learner monitors own learning — self-assessment, peer assessment, reflection, metacognition
  • Assessment OF Learning: Judge achievement after completion — Summative, grade, certification
FOR = Teacher improves learning | AS = Learner monitors learning | OF = Institution judges learning
12

Norm-Referenced Evaluation

Performance compared to other learners or norm group.

NRT = Learner vs Other Learners
  • Ranking
  • Selection
  • Relative position
  • Percentile
  • Competitive examination
13

Criterion-Referenced Evaluation

Performance compared to predetermined criterion, competency or mastery standard.

CRT = Learner vs Fixed Standard
  • Mastery
  • Competency
  • Diagnosis
  • Learning outcomes
  • Certification against standards
14

NRT vs CRT

BasisNorm-ReferencedCriterion-Referenced
ComparisonOther learners/groupFixed standard
NatureRelativeAbsolute/domain-based
PurposeRanking and selectionMastery and diagnosis
ResultRank/percentileMastered/not mastered
QuestionWho performed better?What can the learner do?
15

Internal and External Evaluation

  • Internal: Institution/course teacher conducted — internal assignment, class test, presentation, project
  • External: Outside examiner/university/external agency — university exam, external viva, national eligibility test
16

Quantitative and Qualitative Evaluation

  • Quantitative: Numerical — marks, score, grade point, percentile, frequency
  • Qualitative: Descriptive — observation report, narrative feedback, interview, reflective journal, portfolio commentary

18

Rubric

Rubric = Criteria + Levels + Descriptors
  • Analytic Rubric: Each criterion scored separately
  • Holistic Rubric: Overall performance score/level
  • Rubric vs Checklist: Rubric shows quality/level; Checklist shows presence/absence
19

Portfolio Assessment

Systematic collection of learner's work showing learning, effort, achievement and growth.

  • Drafts, final assignments, projects, reflection, teacher feedback, self-assessment, progress evidence
20

Evaluation in CBCS

Choice Based Credit System — Flexibility to choose core, elective/minor and skill-based courses within prescribed framework.

UGC guidelines connect CBCS to student-centric choice, interdisciplinary learning, grading and learner mobility.

21

CBCS Concepts

  • Core Course: Compulsory foundation course
  • Elective Course: Selected from approved basket
  • Skill-Based Course: Practical/professional competencies
  • Credit: Academic workload or learning volume
  • Grade: Performance category (O, A+, A, B+, B, etc.)
  • Grade Point: Numerical value assigned to letter grade
Credit Point = Course Credit × Grade Point
22

SGPA Calculation

SGPA = Σ(Course Credit × Grade Point) ÷ Σ Credits

CGPA is the cumulative SGPA across semesters.

Example: Course A: Credit 4, Grade Point 8 → Credit Point 32; Course B: Credit 3, Grade Point 7 → 21; Total Credit Points = 53; Total Credits = 7; SGPA = 53/7 = 7.57

23

Advantages of CBCS

  • Learner choice
  • Academic flexibility
  • Interdisciplinary learning
  • Skill-based courses
  • Credit accumulation
  • Credit transfer
  • Learner mobility
  • Student-centred education
  • Grading system
24

Limitations of CBCS

  • Timetable complexity
  • Course availability issues
  • Faculty workload
  • Infrastructure requirements
  • Advising needed
  • Institutional standards variation
  • Actual choice may be limited
  • Internal assessment variation

Exam Trap: CBCS does NOT mean unlimited freedom. Choice is within prescribed course baskets, prerequisites and institutional rules.

25

Computer-Based Testing (CBT)

CBT = Digital Test Delivery + Response Capture + Scoring/Processing

UGC-NET has been conducted in CBT mode by NTA since December 2018.

26

Features of CBT

  • Digital question display
  • On-screen timer
  • Question palette
  • Save and next
  • Mark for review
  • Response modification
  • Item randomisation
  • Automated scoring
  • Item bank
  • Audit trail
  • Result processing
  • Response analytics
27

Advantages of CBT

  • Rapid result processing
  • Standardised administration
  • Large-scale testing
  • Reduced paper handling
  • Randomisation
  • Multimedia questions
  • Automated scoring
  • Response-time analysis
  • Accessible display options
  • Efficient data management
28

Limitations of CBT

  • Digital divide
  • Computer anxiety
  • Technical failures
  • Electricity and network problems
  • Cybersecurity concerns
  • Data privacy
  • Identity verification
  • Technical support needed
  • Complex subjective answer scoring
  • Accessibility issues
29

CBT vs CAT

  • Computer-Based Test (CBT): Computer delivers test; may be fixed
  • Internet-Based/Online Test: Delivered via Internet
  • Remote-Proctored Test: Exam from remote location with monitoring
  • Computerized Adaptive Test (CAT): Next question selected based on previous responses or estimated ability
Every adaptive test is computer-based, but every CBT is not adaptive.

Exam Trap: Random questions ≠ Adaptive testing. CBT ≠ testing from home.

30

Item Bank

Organised digital collection of validated questions with metadata:

  • Subject, Unit, Difficulty level, Cognitive level, Correct answer, Discrimination index, Time required

Exam Trap: Large question bank ≠ automatically valid item bank. Quality, syllabus mapping and psychometric properties are essential.

31

Innovations in Evaluation Systems

  • Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation: Throughout learning, multiple outcomes
  • Outcome-Based Assessment: Evidence mapped to Course/Programme Outcomes
  • Authentic Assessment: Realistic and meaningful tasks
  • Performance-Based Assessment: Demonstrate skill/performance
  • Open-Book Examination: Focus on application, analysis, evaluation, problem-solving
  • Self-Assessment: Learner judges own performance
  • Peer Assessment: Learners give feedback to peers
  • E-Portfolio: Digital evidence of progress
  • Learning Analytics: Analyse learner data for patterns, at-risk identification, personalised feedback
  • AI-Assisted Evaluation: Item drafting, automated feedback, response classification, adaptive testing (with ethical concerns: bias, privacy, transparency, human accountability)
AI should support professional judgement, not eliminate accountability.

33

CIPP Model

CIPP = Context + Input + Process + Product
  • Context: Needs, problems, objectives — What should be done?
  • Input: Resources, strategies, alternatives — How should it be done?
  • Process: Programme implementation monitoring — Is it being done as planned?
  • Product: Outcomes and impact evaluation — Did it succeed?
34

Kirkpatrick's Four-Level Model

  • Reaction: How did participants feel about the training?
  • Learning: What did they learn?
  • Behaviour: What change occurred in workplace behaviour?
  • Results: What impact on the organisation?

One-Page Revision

Evaluation = Evidence + Interpretation + Judgement + Decision
Placement = Entry Level | Diagnostic = Specific Gap and Cause | Formative = During + Feedback + Improvement | Summative = End + Grade + Certification
FOR = Improve Learning | AS = Self-Monitoring | OF = Final Judgement
NRT = Learner vs Group | CRT = Learner vs Standard
Validity = Right Measurement | Reliability = Consistent Measurement | Objectivity = Minimum Scorer Bias
Credit = Workload | Grade Point = Performance Value | SGPA = Σ(Credit × Grade Point) ÷ ΣCredits
CBT = Computer-Delivered Test | CAT = Ability-Based Item Selection

CIPP = Context + Input + Process + Product | Kirkpatrick = Reaction + Learning + Behaviour + Results

Final Exam Approach: For any evaluation question, identify: (1) When is it happening? (2) Compared to what? (3) What decision will the evidence support?
