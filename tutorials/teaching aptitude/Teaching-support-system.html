Teaching Support System: Traditional, Modern & ICT-Based

Concept of Teaching Support System

Teaching Support System से आशय उन resources, materials, technologies, personnel और institutional arrangements से है जो teaching-learning process को अधिक clear, engaging, accessible और effective बनाते हैं।

Teaching Support System = Human Resources + Learning Materials + Technology + Infrastructure + Academic Support

Teaching support system teacher का replacement नहीं है। यह teacher और learner को learning objectives प्राप्त करने में सहायता करता है।

Teaching Support System = Traditional + Modern + ICT-Based + Human/Institutional Support
2

Objectives of Teaching Support System

  • Abstract concepts को concrete बनाना
  • Learner attention और interest बढ़ाना
  • Multiple senses को involve करना
  • Individual differences को address करना
  • Learning को interactive बनाना
  • Difficult concepts की visualisation करना
  • Immediate feedback उपलब्ध कराना
  • Large और diverse groups तक content पहुँचाना
  • Self-paced learning को support करना
  • Inclusive और accessible learning उपलब्ध कराना
  • Assessment तथा learner progress track करना
  • Teacher के समय और effort का effective use करना
3

Traditional Teaching Support System

Traditional Support = Talk and Chalk + Print + Physical Objects + Face-to-Face Interaction
  • Printed Materials: Textbooks, reference books, handouts, worksheets, newspapers, journals, question banks, study guides
  • Display Materials: Blackboard, green board, whiteboard, bulletin board, flannel board, charts, posters, maps, graphs, flashcards
  • Three-Dimensional: Models, specimens, globes, dioramas, real objects, mock-ups
  • Institutional Support: Library, laboratory, tutorial, seminar, workshop, field visit, academic counselling
4

Blackboard/Chalkboard

  • Effective Use: Clear and readable writing, proper letter size, headings/subheadings, avoid overcrowding, underline/highlight important points, simple and labelled diagrams
  • Advantages: Economical, easily available, flexible, immediate explanation, step-by-step development, no electricity needed, useful for mathematical derivations
  • Limitations: Visibility issues in large classrooms, not permanent, time-consuming writing, no complex animation/multimedia, handwriting affects learning
5

Printed Teaching Materials

  • Clear language, logical sequence, appropriate examples, readable typography, learning objectives, self-check questions, summary, references, adequate white space, inclusive and unbiased content

Exam Point: Textbook and teacher are not the same concepts. Textbook is a learning resource; teacher contextualises, explains and adapts content.

6

Classification of Teaching Aids

  • Audio Aids: Radio, podcast, audio recording, language laboratory, recorded speech
  • Visual Aids: Chart, map, diagram, graph, flashcard, poster, model, blackboard
  • Audio-Visual Aids: Television, educational video, film, video lecture, multimedia presentation, interactive video
7

Projected and Non-Projected Aids

  • Non-Projected: Blackboard, chart, map, poster, flashcard, model, specimen, globe, diorama, real object, bulletin board
  • Projected: Overhead projector (OHP), slide projector, filmstrip projector, epidiascope, opaque projector, LCD/digital projector
Chart, Model, Map, Diorama → Non-Projected | OHP, Slide, Filmstrip, Epidiascope → Projected
8

Hardware and Software Approach

  • Hardware Approach (Technology in Education): Physical machines and electronic devices — Radio, television, computer, projector, smartboard, camera, language laboratory, tablet, interactive display
  • Software Approach (Technology of Education): Learning psychology, instructional design, teaching strategies, behavioural principles — Programmed learning, lesson planning, reinforcement, task analysis, instructional design, teaching models, microteaching, formative feedback

Exam Trap: "Software approach" does NOT mean only computer software. It also includes instructional design and learning principles.

9

Modern Teaching Support System

  • Smart classroom, interactive whiteboard, multimedia projector, document camera, digital presentation, simulation, language laboratory, virtual laboratory, multimedia laboratory, video conferencing, digital library, e-books, assistive technology, audience-response system, blended learning environment
10

Smart Classroom

  • Components: Computer/laptop, Internet, projector, interactive board, speakers, digital content, document camera, LMS integration, recording tools, response system
  • Advantages: Multimedia explanation, better visualisation, interactive learning, recorded resources, immediate assessment, wider range of examples, learner engagement
  • Limitations: Cost, technical failure, training requirement, maintenance, digital distraction, overdependence on presentations, unequal accessibility

Exam Trap: Smartboard presence does not automatically make the classroom "smart." Technology is meaningful only when it supports learning objectives, interaction and feedback.

11

Multimedia Learning

Multimedia = Text + Visual + Audio + Animation/Video + Interaction
  • Uses: Complex process visualisation, virtual demonstration, language learning, scientific simulation, historical reconstruction, self-paced learning
  • Cognitive Load Principle: Too much text, animation, sound and decorative elements can overload working memory. Keep relevant content, reduce unnecessary decoration, chunk information, coordinate visual and narration, give processing time.

Exam Trap: More multimedia does not necessarily mean more learning.

12

ICT-Based Teaching Support System

ICT = Information and Communication Technology

ICT Support = Device + Connectivity + Digital Content + Interaction + Assessment + Data
  • Computer and mobile devices, Internet and Wi-Fi, LMS, e-content, digital library, MOOCs, virtual classroom, video conferencing, online assessment, discussion forum, cloud storage, learning analytics, AI-supported tools, assistive technologies
13

Computer-Assisted Instruction (CAI)

  • Drill and Practice: Repeated practice with immediate feedback
  • Tutorial: Step-by-step concept explanation
  • Simulation: Digital model of real situations
  • Educational Game: Learning objectives with rules, challenges, rewards
  • Problem-Solving: Using digital tools to solve problems

Exam Trap: CAI is not a complete replacement for the teacher. The teacher remains important for learning objectives, context, feedback and learner support.

14

Learning Management System (LMS)

Software platform for organising, delivering, tracking and assessing courses and learning activities.

  • Functions: Course content upload, learner enrolment, assignment submission, online quiz, discussion forum, attendance, gradebook, progress tracking, feedback, communication, learning analytics
  • Examples: Moodle, Canvas, Blackboard Learn, Google Classroom, Schoology
ToolPrimary Purpose
LMSCourse delivery and management
CMSContent creation/management
Video-conferencingLive communication
Survey toolResponses and polls
Digital repositoryStorage and access

Exam Point: Moodle and Canvas are LMS. Mentimeter is an interactive presentation/polling tool. Canva is a graphic-design tool, not an LMS.

15

Digital Library and E-Resources

  • Resources: E-books, e-journals, databases, theses and dissertations, institutional repositories, audio-video archives, open educational resources
  • Benefits: Remote access, keyword search, simultaneous access, updated resources, storage efficiency, research support
  • Limitations: Subscription cost, copyright restrictions, digital literacy, screen fatigue, connectivity, information overload
16

Open Educational Resources (OER)

OER are teaching-learning-research resources available under an open licence, allowing reuse or adaptation as per licence conditions.

5R Activities: Retain → Reuse → Revise → Remix → Redistribute

Important Trap: Every resource appearing free on the Internet is not OER. An appropriate open licence is required for OER.

17

Virtual Classroom

  • Synchronous: Live Google Meet/Zoom session, real-time chat, live poll, immediate feedback
  • Asynchronous: Recorded lecture, discussion forum, e-mail, LMS assignment, downloadable notes

Exam Trap: Virtual classroom is not just video conferencing. It includes content, communication, activities, assessment and feedback.

18

Virtual Laboratory and Simulation

  • Applications: Dangerous experiments, expensive equipment, repeated practice, remote learning, pre-lab preparation, abstract scientific processes
  • Limitations: Limited tactile/real equipment experience, simulation is simplification, technical access required, not all practical competencies can be assessed digitally
19

ICT-Based Assessment

  • Online quizzes, automatic scoring, immediate feedback, item banks, randomisation, adaptive testing, e-portfolios, progress tracking, learning analytics, online proctoring, digital rubrics

Exam Trap: AI or automated assessment cannot completely replace all human judgement. Human oversight is required for complex performance, ethical judgement and subjective responses.

20

Assistive Technology and Inclusion

  • Screen reader, text-to-speech, speech-to-text, closed captions, sign-language video, keyboard navigation, magnification, alternative text, accessible documents, adjustable playback speed

Exam Principle: Same content = equality; accessible format = equity and inclusion.

Teaching Support and Blended Learning

Blended Learning = Face-to-Face Strength + Online Flexibility

Example: LMS video + reading → classroom discussion → online quiz → face-to-face feedback → e-portfolio

Talk and chalk + printed notes → Traditional | Projector presentation → ICT-supported | Case-study discussion → Modern/learner-oriented | LMS video + face-to-face discussion → Blended/Hybrid
23

Principles for Selecting Teaching Support

  • Relevance: Related to learning objective and content
  • Learner Appropriateness: Age, prior knowledge, language, disability, digital literacy
  • Simplicity: Should not itself cause confusion
  • Accuracy: Factually and conceptually correct
  • Visibility and Audibility: All learners can see/hear
  • Accessibility: Captions, alternative text, readable font, accessible format
  • Interactivity: Learners can respond, practise, create
  • Cost-Effectiveness: Learning benefit proportionate to cost
  • Reliability: Backup and technical support available
  • Evaluation: Check if support actually improved learning
24

Dale's Cone of Experience

  • Concrete Side: Direct purposeful experiences, contrived experiences, demonstrations, field trips
  • Intermediate: Exhibits, television, motion pictures, recordings and photographs
  • Abstract Side: Visual symbols, verbal symbols

Important Exam Trap: Dale's original Cone should NOT be attached to fixed retention percentages like "we remember 10% of what we read and 90% of what we do." The Cone shows concreteness-abstraction of experience, not a universal retention formula.

25

Applications in Education and Research

  • Education: Complex content visualisation, differentiated instruction, self-paced learning, formative assessment, flipped classroom, inclusive education, remote access, collaborative learning, practical simulation, course management
  • Research: Digital literature search, reference management, online surveys, statistical analysis, data visualisation, plagiarism checking, research collaboration, institutional repositories, webinar/conference, research-data management

Research Trap: Technology can make research faster, but does not automatically ensure validity, ethics, sampling quality or researcher judgement.

26

Important Exam Points

  • Teaching support system is NOT a replacement for the teacher.
  • Blackboard, charts and models are traditional/non-projected aids.
  • OHP, slide projector and epidiascope are projected aids.
  • Hardware approach = machines and devices.
  • Software approach = instructional principles and processes.
  • Smartboard = modern teaching support.
  • ICT = Information and Communication Technology.
  • LMS is useful for delivery, communication, assessment and tracking.
  • Moodle and Canvas are LMS.
  • Canva is NOT an LMS.
  • Mentimeter is an interactive polling/presentation tool.
  • Virtual lab is NOT a complete replacement for a real laboratory.
  • OER requires an open licence.
  • Internet free material is NOT automatically OER.
  • Multimedia overload can reduce learning.
  • Blended learning = planned integration of face-to-face and online.
  • Assistive technology supports inclusive education.
  • ICT-based assessment does NOT eliminate the need for human oversight.
  • Technology selection should be based on learning objectives.
  • Teaching aid effectiveness depends on use, not just modernity.

Common Exam Traps

TrapCorrect Understanding
Traditional aids are always ineffective.They can be highly effective in appropriate contexts.
Modern equipment guarantees learning.Pedagogy and learner engagement are also required.
Software approach means computer applications only.It also includes instructional design and learning principles.
Every visual aid is projected.Charts, maps and models are non-projected.
LMS is hardware.LMS is a software platform.
Projector is an LMS.Projector is display hardware.
Google Meet is a complete LMS.It is primarily a video-conferencing tool.
Every free web resource is OER.An open licence is required.
Virtual lab completely replaces real lab.Both can be complementary.
More multimedia means better learning.Excessive media can cause cognitive overload.
ICT removes the teacher's role.The teacher's role becomes facilitator and instructional designer.
AI can replace every assessment decision.Human validation and oversight are required.

Verified PYQs

PYQ 1: UGC NET, 13 Oct 2022, Shift 2

Statement I: LMS provides virtual environment for teachers and students.
Statement II: LMS can be used in both blended and online learning.
Answer: Both statements are correct.

PYQ 2: UGC NET, 20 Oct 2022, Shift 2

Question: For online content, assessment and virtual teacher-learner interaction, which should be used?
Answer: Learning Management System

PYQ 3: UGC NET, 1 Oct 2022, Shift 2

Correct statements about LMS: It is software, useful for virtual student-teacher groups, can conduct online quizzes. LMS is NOT hardware.

PYQ 4: UGC NET, 14 June 2023, Shift 1

Statement I: Moodle is an LMS.
Statement II: Mentimeter can be used for online surveys/polls.
Answer: Both statements are correct.

PYQ 5: UGC NET, 19 June 2023, Shift 1

LMS examples: Canvas and Moodle. Kahoot is quiz/game-based, Canva is design tool, Padlet is collaborative board.

PYQ 6: UGC NET, 14 March 2023, Shift 1

Statement I: Canvas is an LMS for online teaching-learning.
Statement II: Canvas is only a paid LMS.
Answer: Statement I correct, Statement II false.
