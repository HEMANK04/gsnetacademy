Thesis & Article Writing: Format and Styles of Referencing

Complete tutorial: thesis structure, research article format (IMRaD), APA, MLA, Chicago, Harvard, IEEE, Vancouver styles, citations, references, plagiarism, peer review, PYQs & more.

Meaning of Thesis

Thesis एक विस्तृत, systematic और original academic document है, जिसमें researcher किसी specific problem पर किए गए research, methodology, analysis, findings और conclusions को प्रस्तुत करता है।

Thesis = Research Problem + Literature Review + Methodology + Data Analysis + Findings + Conclusion

यह सामान्यतः किसी academic degree की requirement के रूप में प्रस्तुत की जाती है।

Thesis and Dissertation: इन terms का उपयोग अलग-अलग देशों और universities में अलग हो सकता है। कुछ systems में Master’s के लिए Thesis और Doctoral degree के लिए Dissertation कहा जाता है; अन्य systems में यह distinction उलटा या interchangeable हो सकता है।

2

Research Article

Research Article = Concise Report of Original Research

Research Article किसी original research study को journal के निर्धारित format में concise और structured रूप से प्रस्तुत करने वाला scholarly paper है।

  • एक specific research problem पर केन्द्रित
  • Thesis की तुलना में संक्षिप्त
  • Journal guidelines के अनुसार formatted
  • Abstract और keywords युक्त
  • Method और findings स्पष्ट
  • Citations और references शामिल
  • Publication से पहले सामान्यतः peer review
  • Original contribution अपेक्षित

Characteristics of Scientific Writing

  • Clarity — स्पष्टता: Language easily understood by intended readers
  • Precision — परिशुद्धता: Exact information, not vague expressions
  • Objectivity — वस्तुनिष्ठता: Personal likes/dislikes and unsupported opinions avoided
  • Conciseness — संक्षिप्तता: Unnecessary repetition and decorative language avoided
  • Coherence — सुसंगति: Logical connection between paragraphs and sections
  • Evidence-based Writing: Every important claim based on evidence, data or citation
  • Consistency: One referencing style, heading pattern, terminology, formatting convention throughout
  • Ethical Attribution: Proper citation of others' ideas, words and data
5

Thesis Format

Preliminary Pages: Title page, Declaration, Certificate, Acknowledgements, Abstract, Table of Contents, List of Tables, List of Figures, List of Abbreviations

Main Body: Chapter 1: Introduction (background, problem, objectives, questions, hypotheses, definitions, delimitations, limitations); Chapter 2: Review of Literature; Chapter 3: Research Methodology (approach, design, population, sample, tools, reliability, validity, procedure, ethics, analysis); Chapter 4: Data Analysis and Results; Chapter 5: Discussion; Chapter 6: Summary, Conclusions and Recommendations

End Matter: References, Bibliography (if required), Appendices (questionnaire, consent form, additional tables, relevant documents)

6

IMRaD Format

IMRaD = Introduction – Methods – Results – Discussion
  • Title: Specific, concise, shows main variables/population, no misleading claims
  • Abstract: Self-contained summary (Background, Objective, Method, Results, Conclusion)
  • Keywords: Specific terms for searchability
  • Introduction: Broad Context → Existing Knowledge → Gap → Present Study
  • Methods: Research design, participants, sampling, tools, procedure, ethical approval, analysis techniques
  • Results: Findings presented (tables, figures, statistical results, qualitative themes)
  • Discussion: Interpretation, comparison with previous research, theoretical/practical implications, limitations
  • Conclusion: Concise answer to research question, no new results, no overgeneralisation
7

Title & Abstract

  • Good Title: Specific, concise, shows main variables/population, no unnecessary words, no misleading claims
  • Abstract: Self-contained summary — Problem + Method + Major Results + Conclusion
  • Abstract should NOT contain: Unexplained abbreviations, detailed literature review, new information not in article, unnecessary citations, tables/figures
8

Citation, Reference and Bibliography

  • Citation — उद्धरण-संकेत: Source acknowledged within the main text
  • Reference: Complete publication information of cited source at document end
  • Reference List: Only sources actually cited in the text
  • Bibliography: May include cited sources PLUS other consulted sources not cited in text
Every in-text citation must have a corresponding reference, and every reference-list entry should normally be cited in the text.
9

Purpose of Referencing

  • Credit original authors
  • Avoid plagiarism
  • Provide evidence for claims
  • Enable source verification
  • Enhance academic transparency
  • Connect previous and present research
  • Maintain standardisation

Exam Point: Incorrect referencing is not just a formatting problem; it can be a technical lapse, academic negligence, plagiarism, or research misconduct depending on context.

11

APA Style

APA In-text Citation = Author + Year

Paraphrase: Teaching effectiveness is related to learner engagement (Sharma, 2024).

Narrative Citation: Sharma (2024) found a relationship between teaching effectiveness and learner engagement.

Direct Quotation: (Sharma, 2024, p. 25) — page number required when available.

APA Journal Reference: Author. (Year). Article title. Journal Title, Volume(Issue), page range. DOI

Example: Sharma, R. (2024). Digital pedagogy and learner engagement. Journal of Educational Research, 18(2), 45–62. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxx

  • Journal title italic
  • Volume number italic
  • Issue number in parentheses, non-italic
  • DOI at the end
  • Article title in sentence case
  • Reference list in alphabetical order by author surname

Exam Trap: In APA 7, publisher location is generally not included and DOI is presented in URL format.

12

MLA Style

MLA In-text Citation = Author + Page Number

Example: Experiential learning increases participation (Sharma 42).

MLA Book Pattern: Author’s Surname, First Name. Title of Book. Publisher, Year.

Final list is called Works Cited.

APAMLA
Author + YearAuthor + Page
Education/social sciencesLiterature/humanities
ReferencesWorks Cited
13

Chicago Style

  • Notes and Bibliography: Footnotes/endnotes — common in history and humanities
  • Author–Date: Parenthetical author–year citation — used in social and natural sciences

Exam Trap: Chicago is NOT only footnote-based; it also has an Author–Date system.

14

Harvard Style

Harvard is an author–date referencing system.

In-text Example: (Sharma, 2024)

General Pattern: Author, Year. Title. Publication details.

APA vs Harvard: Both are author–date systems, but punctuation, capitalisation and formatting rules may differ.

15

IEEE and Vancouver

  • IEEE: Engineering and technical disciplines — In-text citation: [4]; references numbered in citation order
  • Vancouver: Medicine and biomedical sciences — numbered style
IEEE/Vancouver = Numbered Citation | APA/Harvard = Author–Date | MLA = Author–Page
16

DOI, ISBN and ISSN

  • DOI — Digital Object Identifier: Persistent identifier for digital scholarly objects (especially journal articles). Unique; stable identification even if URL changes; comes at the end of APA journal reference.
  • ISBN — International Standard Book Number: Identifies books and book editions
  • ISSN — International Standard Serial Number: Identifies journals, magazines and serial publications
DOI → Digital Object | ISBN → Book | ISSN → Journal/Serial
17

Exam Trap: ibid. only refers to the immediately preceding citation. et al. means "and others," not "editor."

18

Tables and Figures

  • Table: Numerical or textual information in rows and columns
  • Figure: Graph, chart, photograph, diagram, map or illustration
  • Number each table/figure
  • Give a clear title
  • Mention in the text
  • Cite source if adapted/reproduced
  • Do not unnecessarily repeat the same data in text, table and figure
  • Table/figure should be self-explanatory
19

Peer Review and Article Publication

Manuscript Preparation → Journal Selection → Submission → Editorial Screening → Peer Review → Revision → Acceptance/Rejection → Publication
  • Peer Review: Field experts examine manuscript for originality, methodological soundness, accuracy, relevance, clarity, ethical compliance
  • Types: Single-blind, Double-blind, Open peer review

Exam Point: Peer review strengthens quality control but does not guarantee error-free publication.

20

Plagiarism

Presenting another person's words, ideas, images, data or work as one's own without appropriate acknowledgement.

  • Types: Direct, Mosaic/patchwork, Inadequate paraphrasing, Source-based, Self-plagiarism, Accidental
  • Prevention: Proper citation, accurate quotation marks, correct paraphrasing, complete reference list, record sources in note-taking, responsible use of plagiarism-detection tools, cite figures/tables/data

Important Trap: Changing only a few words is NOT proper paraphrasing. Proper paraphrasing means genuinely expressing the idea in your own words and structure while maintaining the original meaning — AND citing the source.

21

Exam Traps

  • APA = American Psychological Association (Author–Year)
  • MLA = Author–Page
  • IEEE = Numbers in square brackets
  • DOI comes at the end of APA journal reference
  • Reference list ≠ Bibliography
  • Abstract ≠ Introduction
  • Results ≠ Discussion
  • Thesis is detailed; article is condensed
  • Scientific writing is precise, not ornamental
  • Footnote at page bottom; Endnote at document/chapter end
  • Incorrect citation can constitute plagiarism
  • Peer review improves quality but doesn't guarantee perfect accuracy
  • Journal impact factor is not the final proof of individual article quality
