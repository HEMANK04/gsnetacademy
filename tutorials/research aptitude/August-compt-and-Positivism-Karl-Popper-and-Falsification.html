Auguste Comte, Positivism & Post-Positivism (Karl Popper & Falsification)

Complete tutorial: Auguste Comte, Positivism, Law of Three Stages, Hierarchy of Sciences, Karl Popper, Falsification, Verification, Triangulation, PYQs & more.

Master Association

Auguste Comte → Positivism → Observation → Measurement → Scientific Method
Karl Popper → Post-Positivism → Falsification → Critical Testing → Tentative Knowledge
2

Auguste Comte

Auguste Comte (1798–1857) — French philosopher, known as Father of Positivism and Father of Sociology.

Comte = Sociology + Positivism + Scientific Study of Society + Law of Three Stages

Reliable knowledge should be based on observation, comparison, classification, experiment and scientific reasoning — not theological speculation or abstract metaphysics.

3

Meaning of Positivism

Positivism = Observation + Measurement + Objectivity + Verification + Generalisation

Positivism: Philosophical and research approach where authentic knowledge is based on observable, measurable and empirically verifiable facts. Emphasises using natural science methods for studying social phenomena.

4

Law of Three Stages

Theological → Metaphysical → Positive
  • Theological Stage: Phenomena explained by supernatural powers, gods, divine will. (Fetishism → Polytheism → Monotheism)
  • Metaphysical Stage: Phenomena explained by abstract forces, essences, speculative concepts (transitional stage).
  • Positive/Scientific Stage: Phenomena explained through observation, evidence, classification and scientific laws.
Theological = Supernatural Cause | Metaphysical = Abstract Cause | Positive = Scientific Evidence
5

Hierarchy of Sciences

Mathematics → Astronomy → Physics → Chemistry → Biology → Sociology
  • Mathematics is most general and comparatively simple
  • Sociology is most complex (study of human society)
  • Later sciences partly depend on earlier sciences' principles

Exam Trap: Comte's original hierarchy did NOT give Psychology a prominent place as an independent science.

6

Positivist Research Assumptions

  • Ontology: Objective reality exists independently of the researcher.
  • Epistemology: Knowledge comes from sensory observation, measurement and empirical evidence.
  • Researcher Role: Detached, neutral, objective, value-free.
  • Methodology: Experiment, survey, structured observation, quantitative measurement, statistical analysis, hypothesis testing, deductive reasoning.
7

Positivist Research Process

Theory → Hypothesis → Operationalisation → Data Collection → Statistical Testing → Conclusion
  • Theory: Expected relationship from existing theory
  • Hypothesis: Testable relationship between variables
  • Operationalisation: Abstract concepts → measurable indicators
  • Data Collection: Numerical data with structured tools
  • Statistical Testing: Hypothesis testing with appropriate statistics
  • Generalisation: Applying sample findings to larger population
8

Characteristics of Positivism

  • Objective reality
  • Empirical observation
  • Measurement and quantification
  • Hypothesis construction
  • Deductive reasoning
  • Cause–effect relationships
  • Prediction and control
  • Researcher neutrality
  • Value-free inquiry
  • Replication
  • Verification
  • General laws
  • Statistical analysis
  • Context-free generalisation
Positivism = Facts + Numbers + Objectivity + Law-like Explanation
9

Positivism and Quantitative Research

Positivist PrincipleQuantitative Application
Objective realityStandardised measurement
ObservationEmpirical data
QuantificationNumerical scores
Hypothesis testingStatistical tests
GeneralisationRepresentative sample
ReplicationStandardised procedure

Exam Trap: Not every quantitative study is philosophically pure positivist. Post-positivists also use quantitative methods.

10

Verification Principle

Verification = Supporting a claim through observable evidence

In traditional positivist orientation, proposition's meaning and scientific value are linked to empirical observation/verification.

Limitation: Many observations supporting a theory do NOT prove it permanently and universally true. This limitation was important in the development of Post-Positivism and Popper's falsification approach.

11

Limitations of Positivism

  • Complete objectivity is difficult
  • Neglects human meaning (intention, emotion, culture, identity, experience)
  • Under-emphasises context
  • Measurement error exists
  • Observation is theory-laden
  • Universal laws are difficult in social reality
12

Post-Positivism

Post-Positivism = Reality Exists, but Our Knowledge Is Partial, Fallible and Revisable
  • Does not completely reject Positivism
  • Accepts scientific inquiry but modifies claims of complete certainty, perfect objectivity and final truth
  • Main Associations: Karl Popper, Critical Realism, Falsification, Fallibilism, Probabilistic knowledge, Theory revision, Triangulation, Critical examination
13

Critical Realism

  • Reality exists independently of the researcher
  • Reality cannot be perfectly known
  • Observation and measurement may contain error
  • Knowledge is tentative and revisable
  • Multiple methods can improve understanding
Reality is real, but our access to it is imperfect.
14

Fallibilism

Any scientific conclusion may be revised in light of better evidence.

Post-positivist researcher presents conclusions as:

  • Best available explanation
  • Tentative conclusion
  • Probabilistic statement
15

Karl Popper and Falsification

Scientific Claim = Testable + Falsifiable

A scientific theory should be capable of being proven false by evidence.

Conjecture → Critical Test → Refutation/Support → Theory Revision

Exam Trap: Falsification does NOT mean the researcher deliberately creates false data. It means subjecting the theory to severe critical tests.

16

Verification vs Falsification

VerificationFalsification
Seeks supporting evidenceSeeks disconfirming evidence
Emphasis on confirmationEmphasis on critical testing
Positivist tendencyPopper/Post-positivist tendency
Supports the theoryTests theory's weaknesses
Risk of final certaintyAccepts tentative knowledge
A universal claim cannot be conclusively verified by observing all possible cases, but one clear contradictory case can falsify it.
17

Post-Positivist Research Process

Theory → Tentative Hypothesis → Rigorous Test → Evidence → Critical Interpretation → Revision → Retesting
  • Examines alternative explanations
  • Acknowledges measurement error
  • Attempts to reduce bias
  • Presents findings in probabilistic language
  • Remains open to theory revision
18

Objectivity in Post-Positivism

Objectivity is an ideal to be approached, not a guaranteed state.
  • Standardised procedures
  • Peer review
  • Replication
  • Reflexivity
  • Bias disclosure
  • Multiple researchers
  • Triangulation
  • Transparent data analysis
  • Alternative hypotheses
19

Triangulation

Studying a phenomenon using multiple methods, sources, researchers or theories.

  • Data Triangulation: Different participants, times, settings
  • Method Triangulation: Survey + Interview + Observation
  • Investigator Triangulation: Multiple researchers analysing
  • Theory Triangulation: Different theoretical perspectives

Exam Trap: Triangulation does NOT guarantee complete truth; it improves credibility and completeness.

20

Post-Positivism and Methods

Post-positivism should NOT be equated only with qualitative research.

  • Quantitative methods
  • Qualitative methods
  • Mixed methods
  • Experiments, surveys, interviews
  • Triangulation
  • Multiple measurements

The main issue is NOT the method, but the philosophical position about knowledge.

21

Exam Caution: In older UGC NET questions, phenomenology, ethnography and action research were placed under "post-positivistic approach." In contemporary methodology, they are more commonly associated with interpretivist/constructivist traditions. Follow the official PYQ classification for exam answers.

24

Important Exam Traps

  • Correct spelling: Auguste Comte (NOT "August Compt")
  • Comte → Father of Positivism AND Father of Sociology
  • Positive Stage = Scientific Stage (NOT "optimistic thinking")
  • Positivism = observation, measurement, objectivity
  • Post-positivism does NOT reject reality
  • Post-positivism rejects claims of perfect knowledge and complete objectivity
  • Popper → Falsification (NOT verification)
  • Verification and Falsification are NOT the same
  • Post-positivism is NOT only qualitative
  • Triangulation does NOT guarantee truth; it improves credibility
  • Observation is NOT always completely theory-free
  • Post-positivist conclusions = tentative/probabilistic (NOT final truth)
  • PYQ classification vs contemporary classification may differ for phenomenology/ethnography

One-Page Revision

Auguste Comte = Positivism + Sociology + Law of Three Stages
Three Stages = Theological → Metaphysical → Positive
Positive Stage = Observation + Evidence + Science
Positivism = Objective Reality + Measurement + Verification
Research Process = Theory → Hypothesis → Data → Statistical Test
Post-Positivism = Critical Realism + Fallibility + Theory Revision
Karl Popper = Falsification
Falsification = Search for Evidence that Can Refute a Claim
Triangulation = Multiple Methods/Sources/Perspectives
Positivism seeks certainty; Post-Positivism accepts provisional knowledge.

Final Exam Formula: Comte → Positivism + Sociology + Three Stages | Popper → Falsification + Critical Testing + Tentative Knowledge
