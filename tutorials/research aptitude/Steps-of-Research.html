Research Process Overview

Research Process = Problem → Literature → Objectives → Hypothesis → Design → Sample → Data → Analysis → Interpretation → Report

Research एक systematic, logical और evidence-based process है। Research हमेशा पूरी तरह linear नहीं होता; qualitative और exploratory research में researcher पिछले चरणों पर वापस जाकर बदलाव कर सकता है।

2

Identification of Research Problem

Research Problem वह specific issue, difficulty, knowledge gap या unanswered question है जिसका systematic investigation किया जाना है।

  • Characteristics: Clear, specific, researchable, relevant, feasible, ethical, aligned with researcher's competence/interest
  • Sources: Previous research gaps, existing theories, classroom/workplace problems, social/educational issues, contradictory findings, personal experience, government reports, emerging trends

Exam Trap: Research area and research problem are not the same. "Teaching Aptitude" is an area; "Effect of flipped classroom on achievement" is a problem.

3

Review of Related Literature

Literature Review = What is known + What is not known + What needs investigation
  • Primary Sources: Original research articles, theses, dissertations, original reports, conference papers, original datasets
  • Secondary Sources: Review articles, textbooks, encyclopaedias, research summaries, meta-analysis reports
  • Purpose: Understand previous research, identify research gaps, clarify concepts/theories, avoid unnecessary duplication, select methods/tools, support hypothesis building, provide background for comparison
4

Formulation of Objectives

  • Characteristics: Specific, clear, measurable/observable, aligned with research problem, feasible
  • Action Verbs: To identify, examine, compare, determine, analyse, explore, evaluate

Exam Trap: Objective and method are different. "Comparing achievement" is an objective; using t-test for comparison is an analysis technique.

6

Formulation of Hypothesis

Hypothesis = Tentative + Testable Prediction
  • Null Hypothesis (H₀): No relationship or difference exists
  • Alternative/Research Hypothesis (H₁): Relationship or difference exists
  • Directional: Specifies the direction of relationship/difference
  • Non-directional: States difference without specifying direction

Exam Trap: Every research does NOT require a formal hypothesis. Exploratory and some qualitative studies may use research questions only.

7

Variables & Operational Definition

  • Independent Variable: Manipulated by researcher
  • Dependent Variable: Measured outcome
  • Extraneous Variable: Other factor affecting outcome
  • Control Variable: Deliberately kept constant
  • Confounding Variable: Changes with IV and obscures results
Conceptual Definition = Meaning | Operational Definition = How to measure

Example: Academic achievement = score on a standardised achievement test (operational definition).

8

Selection of Research Design

Research Design = Blueprint for Data Collection, Measurement and Analysis
  • Major Designs: Experimental, Descriptive, Survey, Correlational, Historical, Case study, Qualitative, Ex-post facto, Mixed methods
9

Population and Sample

Population → Sampling → Sample
  • Probability Sampling: Simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster, multistage
  • Non-probability Sampling: Convenience, purposive, quota, snowball

Exam Point: Large sample ≠ automatically representative. Quantitative research often uses probability sampling; qualitative research commonly uses purposive sampling.

10

Research Tools

  • Common Tools: Questionnaire, Interview schedule, Observation schedule, Achievement test, Aptitude test, Attitude scale, Rating scale, Checklist, Focus-group discussion guide
  • Quality Criteria: Reliability (consistency), Validity (accuracy/appropriateness), Objectivity (minimal scorer bias), Usability (practical)
A tool can be reliable but not valid. A valid tool must be sufficiently reliable.
11

Pilot Study and Pre-testing

  • Pilot Study: Small-scale trial of the main research procedure
  • Purpose: Check feasibility, clarity, identify tool flaws, estimate time, anticipate field problems, gather reliability/validity evidence
  • Pre-test: Specifically testing the questionnaire/tool on a small group for clarity and suitability

Exam Trap: Pilot study is NOT a replacement for the main study; it is preparation for it.

12

Data Collection

  • Primary Data: Collected first-hand by researcher (interviews, questionnaires, observations, experiments, tests)
  • Secondary Data: Already available (census reports, government publications, institutional records, published research, databases)
  • Precautions: Informed consent, confidentiality, standardised procedure, no unnecessary pressure, accurate records, no fabrication/falsification
13

Organisation and Processing of Data

Raw Data → Editing → Coding → Classification → Tabulation
  • Editing: Checking incomplete, inconsistent, incorrect responses
  • Coding: Assigning symbols/numbers to responses (e.g., Male=1, Female=2)
  • Classification: Grouping similar data into categories
  • Tabulation: Presenting data in rows and columns
  • Data Cleaning: Checking missing values, duplicates, outliers
14

Data Analysis

  • Descriptive Statistics: Frequency, percentage, mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation
  • Inferential Statistics: t-test, ANOVA, Chi-square, correlation, regression
  • Qualitative Analysis: Transcription, coding, categorisation, theme development, pattern identification, narrative/content analysis, interpretation

Exam Trap: Analysis ≠ Interpretation. Analysis shows what the data says; Interpretation explains the meaning of the findings.

15

Hypothesis Testing

  • Common Significance Levels: 0.05, 0.01
  • Process: Formulate H₀ → Set significance level → Select test → Calculate statistic → Compare p-value/critical value → Reject or fail to reject H₀
If evidence is insufficient: "Fail to reject the null hypothesis" (NOT "accept" it as proven true).
16

Interpretation of Findings

  • Connect findings to objectives
  • Explain results in context of hypotheses
  • Compare with previous studies
  • Discuss theoretical and practical implications
  • Provide possible explanations for unexpected findings

Example: r=0.60 between study time and achievement → moderately strong positive relationship, but does NOT prove causation.

17

Conclusion and Generalisation

  • Conclusion: Concise, evidence-based answer to research questions
  • Generalisation: Applying sample findings to wider population

Exam Trap: Not every study can be universally generalised. Qualitative research emphasises transferability rather than statistical generalisation.

18

Recommendations and Implications

  • Practical actions based on findings
  • Suggestions for future research
  • Should be realistic and connected to findings
19

Research Report Writing

  • Preliminary Pages: Title page, declaration, acknowledgement, abstract, table of contents, list of tables/figures
  • Main Body: Introduction, Literature review, Methodology, Results, Discussion, Findings & conclusions, Recommendations, Limitations
  • End Matter: References/Bibliography, Appendices, Research tools, Consent forms
  • Qualities: Clarity, logical organisation, academic language, accurate citations, objectivity, transparency, plagiarism-free
20

Dissemination and Follow-up

  • Channels: Journal article, conference presentation, thesis/dissertation, policy brief, institutional report, seminar/workshop, research repository
Research is cyclical: One study often generates another research problem.

22

Important Exam Traps

  • Research problem selection begins BEFORE literature review, but review may refine the problem
  • Hypothesis is NOT mandatory in every research
  • Pilot study is NOT the main research
  • Reliability does NOT guarantee validity
  • Research design and tool selection must occur BEFORE data collection
  • Analysis and interpretation are DIFFERENT steps
  • Correlation does NOT prove causation
  • Better to say "fail to reject null hypothesis" than "accept" it
  • Report writing is the final formal step, but dissemination follows
  • Research process is NOT rigidly linear; it can be iterative
